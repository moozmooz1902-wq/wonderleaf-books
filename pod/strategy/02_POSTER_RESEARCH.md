# 02 — Poster research: Redbubble, Displate, Etsy, Society6

## There are two poster markets, and they need opposite strategies

| | **A. Collector / fandom wall** | **B. Décor / gallery wall** |
|---|---|---|
| Where | Displate, Redbubble wall art | Etsy (physical + printable), Society6, Redbubble |
| Buyer | Fan, gamer, collector — buys *a subject* | Decorator — buys *a look for a wall* |
| Searches by | Franchise, character, subject | Style + colour + room + size |
| Price tolerance | High (premium metal, $60–120) | $5–35 printable, $20–60 framed |
| Emotional job | `fandom_shrine`, `nostalgia_hit` | `room_decor` |
| Design driver | One iconic moment, high drama | Palette harmony, negative space, sets |
| **IP reality** | **Mostly licensed — largely closed to us** | **Wide open** |

Getting this split wrong is the single biggest source of wasted output. Décor
rules applied to a collector poster produce something forgettable; collector
drama applied to a décor poster produces something nobody hangs above a sofa.

## Finding A: Displate's top sellers are almost entirely licensed IP

Displate's published Top 25 best-selling designs of 2025 is dominated by
franchise work — Avatar, Lord of the Rings, Arcane/League of Legends, Star Wars
(multiple), Cyberpunk 2077, One Piece, Elden Ring, Dark Souls, Harry Potter,
Fallout, Warhammer 40K, Baldur's Gate 3, Call of Duty, Marvel. **Nineteen of
the twenty-five are licensed. Six are original work.**

**Strategic implication:** the visible top of that market is not reachable by an
unlicensed seller. Chasing it is how POD accounts get banned. Any "research the
best sellers and make more like that" loop will walk straight into infringement,
and I suspect this is part of what made previous batches both ineffective *and*
risky.

**But the originals that did chart tell us exactly what to build.** The
non-licensed entries in that list share a precise profile:

| Design | Profile |
|---|---|
| *Judgmental Duck* | Single character, humour, flat bold, instantly readable |
| *Red Moon Japan* | Minimalist zen — black / white / blood red, extreme contrast |
| *Golden Tree of Harmony* | Sculptural symbol — gold metallic, turquoise, marble |
| *Space Fantasy Landscape* | Ethereal blues, galactic gradient, serene scale |
| *Cherry Blossom Japan* | Electric pink + deep blue, fantasy landscape |
| *Starry Night and the Cat* | **Public-domain** fine art fused with a modern motif |

The pattern, in one sentence: **one focal subject, an extreme and named palette,
a feeling rather than a scene, and total legibility at thumbnail size.**

The *Starry Night and the Cat* entry is the most useful lesson in the list —
public-domain masters (Van Gogh, Hokusai, Klimt, Mucha, Monet, Hiroshige,
Audubon, Redouté botanicals, vintage US National Park / WPA posters) are a
legitimate, high-demand, zero-IP-risk source of exactly the "recognisable
subject" advantage that licensed work has. Fuse a public-domain composition with
a modern motif and you get fandom-grade recognition without fandom-grade risk.

Displate's own category list — Anime, Gaming & Fantasy, Movies & Shows, Cars,
Nature & Landscapes, Sports, Space, Books, Music, Animals, Comics, Fantasy,
Floral, Japanese & Asian, Inspirational, Travel, Landscapes, Cute — tells you
which *unlicensed* lanes exist: **Cars, Nature & Landscapes, Space, Floral,
Japanese & Asian, Inspirational, Travel, Cute** are all reachable.

## Finding B: the décor market is style-led and has published demand

Etsy printable wall art demand, by rough monthly search volume and price band:

| Niche | Monthly searches | Price band |
|---|---|---|
| Abstract art prints | 90,000+ | $10–35 |
| Minimalist quote prints | 50,000+ | $5–18 |
| Botanical & nature prints | 40,000+ | $8–25 |
| Boho / earthy tone prints | 35,000+ | $10–30 |
| Vintage & retro posters | 30,000+ | $8–25 |
| Nursery wall art | high AOV — multi-print sets | $10–30 |

Named aesthetic movements currently pulling: **Châteaucore** (old-world glamour),
**MIDImalism** (minimalism with one bold element), botanical/nature, minimalist
line art, boho, gallery-wall sets.

### Décor buyers search in a grammar, not in keywords

```
[colour] + [style] + [subject] + [format] + [room]
"beige boho abstract arch print living room"
"sage green botanical line art set of 3"
"black and white minimalist mountain print bedroom"
```

Almost all of our generated metadata has been missing **colour** and **room** —
and those two are what a decorator actually filters on. A title without a colour
word is invisible to half the décor market.

### Sets beat singles
Multi-print sets carry the highest average order value in printable wall art, and
Etsy's own trend reporting shows shoppers building gallery walls. **Design in
families of three** sharing one palette and one style, differing only in subject.
That is a legitimate 3× volume multiplier that does *not* trip duplicate spam
detection, because the subjects genuinely differ.

## Finding C: Redbubble wall art is aesthetic-first, and illustration wins

Redbubble's audience skews young — students and young adults decorating rooms and
dorms. They browse **by mood**, not by precise search phrase. Consequences:

- Illustration outperforms typography in Redbubble wall art (the reverse of the
  t-shirt category).
- The unit of appeal is an **aesthetic label**: cottagecore, dark academia, Y2K,
  coquette, grunge, weirdcore, liminal.
- Redbubble's own strong niches: aesthetic moods, anime/gaming *vibes* (no names),
  meme humour, mental health & self-expression, music-scene aesthetics (no band
  names), niche hobbies & subcultures, animal + aesthetic stacking, activism.
- **Stacking is the technique**: aesthetic × specific motif. Not "cottagecore" —
  *"cottagecore mushroom foraging"*. Not "cat" — *"dark academia black cat"*,
  *"watercolour axolotl"*, *"cottagecore hen"*.

Saturation reads: aesthetic **medium-high**, anime/gaming **high**, meme
**medium-high**, mental health **medium**, music **medium**, animals **medium**,
niche hobbies **low-medium** ← the thinnest competition on the platform.

## Composition rules that survive every platform

1. **One focal subject.** Every original that charted on Displate has exactly one.
2. **Extreme, named contrast.** *Red Moon Japan* is three colours. Muddy mid-tone
   art dies in a thumbnail grid.
3. **200px minimum edge buffer** on all sides for anything that must be read.
4. **Never draw a frame or border into the artwork** — it gets trimmed, and
   Displate rejects for it.
5. **Avoid very dark overall compositions** — an explicit Displate rejection
   reason, and they print badly on metal.
6. **Negative space is a feature in décor**, not wasted canvas — it is what makes
   a print hang well next to other prints.
7. **No upscaling, no grain-masking filters, no soft/blurred backgrounds** — all
   named rejection triggers.
8. **Design at ratio.** 1:1.4 for Displate, 2:3 and 3:4 for posters, 4:5 for the
   16×20 frame market. Cropping a 2:3 file to 1:1.4 destroys the composition;
   generate per-ratio instead.

## Aspect-ratio families to ship

| Ratio | Sizes | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 2:3 | 12×18, 20×30, 24×36 | The dominant US poster ratio |
| 3:4 | 18×24, 30×40 | 18×24 is the commercial framing standard |
| 4:5 | 8×10, 16×20 | Frame-friendly, matches Instagram portrait |
| 11:14 | 11×14 | Common ready-made frame |
| 1:1.414 | A4/A3/A2/A1 | Required for non-US buyers |
| 1:1.4 | Displate | Their native ratio, close to but not identical with A-series |
| 1:1 | Square | Redbubble art-print master canvas (3840×3840) |
