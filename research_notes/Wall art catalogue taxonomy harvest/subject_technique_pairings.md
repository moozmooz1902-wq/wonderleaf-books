# Which technique goes with which subject — measured, not guessed

Across all 1,463,031 listings. "Lift" is how many times more often a subject
and a technique appear together than they would if the pairing were random. A
lift of 1.0 is chance; 10 means ten times more often than chance.

Knowing the subject list and the technique list separately still leaves the
pairing to guesswork. **This is the lookup table that removes it.**

## Read as a generator lookup

**Printmaking subjects — linocut and woodblock.** Birds (×12.4), foxes (×9.5),
whales and sea life (×8.4), leaves and botanicals (×6.9), owls (×6.1). Wildlife
and foliage are what the block-print idiom is *for*, and linocut is the single
most under-supplied technique in the whole corpus — 537 listings across 1.46
million. **British wildlife in linocut is the clearest open seam we have.**

**Paint subjects — oil and impasto.** Horses (×19.4), lakes and rivers (×14.8),
mountains (×6.5), beaches (×6.5), forests (×5.7). Landscape and large animals
want loaded brushwork, not line.

**Drawn subjects — pencil and line.** Dogs (×22.5 pencil), roses (×11.2 line
art), women and portraits (line art ×2.0). Dogs are *drawn*, not painted, and
that is a strong, counter-intuitive signal worth obeying.

**Deco subjects — the drinks cabinet.** Cocktails and wine are art deco at
**×27.7** and mid-century at ×12.8; coffee and tea are art deco at ×6.5. The
association is overwhelming and it is about *era*, not subject: these are
1920s-30s bar-culture objects and the market renders them that way.

**Flat-graphic subjects.** Maps are minimalist at ×14.3 — by far their biggest
single pairing at 6,511 listings. Dinosaurs are silhouettes (×10.3), sunsets
are silhouettes (×5.9).

**Decorative-era subjects — art nouveau.** Cats (×13.6) and birds (×17.6).
Nouveau's whiplash line suits animals with a curve to them.

**Collage subjects.** Flowers (×6.2), whales (×3.0), mushrooms (×3.2),
dinosaurs (×4.1).

**Risograph** barely exists in the corpus (59 listings) but where it appears it
is on **fruit and veg at ×8.9** — the flat, limited-ink, slightly misregistered
look suits simple produce shapes.

## What this changes about Phase 1

Our UK place plan was going to apply the same fifteen treatments across every
place atom. The data says don't:

- **Fells, mountains, valleys → oil / impasto** (×6.5), with minimalist (×3.4)
  and line art (×2.6) as the flat alternatives.
- **Lochs, lakes, rivers, canals → oil / impasto** (×14.8). This is the
  strongest landscape pairing in the whole table and we have 1,013 Scottish
  lochs, 1,205 named rivers and 195 canals.
- **Coast, beaches, cliffs → oil / impasto** (×6.5) then **watercolour**
  (×2.4).
- **Woods and forests → oil / impasto** (×5.7) then vintage plate (×2.2).
- **Maps → minimalist**, always. And minimalist maps need no lettering, which
  makes them the one map format diffusion can carry.
- **British wildlife → linocut**, then watercolour and pencil.
- **Native plants → linocut** (×6.9) or **vintage botanical plate** (×5.4).

## Caveats worth keeping

- This measures **what they made**, not what sold. Lift shows a production
  convention, and conventions can be wrong — but they are a better prior than
  our taste.
- Matching is keyword-based on titles, so a listing whose title omits the
  technique is invisible here. Absolute counts are floors.
- A high lift on a small base is weak evidence: coffee-and-tea art deco is
  ×6.5 on only 58 listings. The pairings above 1,000 listings — map/minimalist,
  bird/linocut, dog/pencil, flower/collage, botanical/vintage plate — are the
  solid ones.
- Technique totals for context: painting 76,895 · abstract 75,084 ·
  illustration 56,833 · vintage 44,552 · watercolour 37,428 · minimalist 28,684
  · retro 15,797 · geometric 13,252 · line art 11,559 · ukiyo-e 11,049 ·
  collage 8,272 · neon 7,527.
