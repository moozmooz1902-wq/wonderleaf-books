# Enumerable Subject Lists for a Wall-Art Catalogue (UK-first)

Scope note: this file is a **list harvest**, not analysis. Every section gives
(a) the authoritative list and its count, (b) the source URL, (c) actual names
(full list where short enough, a 30–50 entry sample plus the subdivision
structure where the list is too long), and (d) an IP-risk flag.

Confidence convention used throughout:
- **[V]** count verified against a source in this session (URL given).
- **[T]** count from the researcher's prior knowledge, source URL given for the
  canonical list but the *count* was not re-verified this session — treat as
  provisional and re-check before the arithmetic is final.
- **[?]** sources disagree or the figure is genuinely contested; both figures given.

## BRITISH AND EUROPEAN FAUNA

### Takeaway
The UK fauna lists are unusually well-bounded and officially maintained: the BOU
British List (636 species) is the single best "subject axis" in the whole
catalogue because it is canonical, downloadable as a spreadsheet with Latin
binomials, and carries zero IP risk. Mammals (107 incl. cetaceans), butterflies
(59), reptiles (6) and amphibians (7) are small, closed lists; moths (2,500+) and
insects are effectively open-ended.

### Cited Findings

**Birds — the BOU British List: 636 species as at 31 December 2025** [V]
— 619 Category A, 7 Category B, 10 Category C. Only Categories A, B and C
constitute the British List. — [BOU British List](https://bou.org.uk/british-list/)
- The BOU publishes the list as a downloadable spreadsheet/PDF with common name,
  scientific name, category, and status. The 10th edition is aligned to
  **AviList 2025** (the new unified global taxonomy) — [BOU British List 10th edition / AviList 2025 PDF](https://bou.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/BOU_British_List_10th-and-58th_AviList_2025_Cat-F.pdf)
- Category definitions (useful as catalogue filters): **A** = recorded in a wild
  state since 1 Jan 1950; **B** = recorded in a wild state 1800–1949 only;
  **C** = naturalised introductions (C1–C6 sub-categories); **D** = origin
  uncertain (NOT on the list); **E** = escapes (NOT on the list);
  **F** = pre-1800 records — [BOURC](https://bou.org.uk/british-list/about-bourc/)
- Subdivision structure for fetching the full 636: the list is ordered
  taxonomically by **order → family**. Orders present on the British List
  (use these as catalogue sub-collections):
  Anseriformes (wildfowl), Galliformes (gamebirds), Podicipediformes (grebes),
  Phoenicopteriformes (flamingos, Cat. E mostly), Columbiformes (pigeons/doves),
  Pterocliformes (sandgrouse), Caprimulgiformes (nightjars),
  Apodiformes (swifts), Cuculiformes (cuckoos), Gruiformes (rails/cranes),
  Otidiformes (bustards), Gaviiformes (divers), Procellariiformes
  (albatrosses/petrels/shearwaters), Ciconiiformes (storks),
  Suliformes (gannets/cormorants), Pelecaniformes (pelicans/herons/egrets/ibises),
  Accipitriformes (raptors), Strigiformes (owls), Bucerotiformes (hoopoe),
  Coraciiformes (kingfishers/bee-eaters/rollers), Piciformes (woodpeckers),
  Falconiformes (falcons), Psittaciformes (parakeets),
  Charadriiformes (waders/gulls/terns/auks), Passeriformes (perching birds —
  the largest order, ~250+ of the 636).

### Inferences
- The BOU British List is the highest-value single axis: 636 named subjects with
  Latin binomials, free of IP risk, and already divided into orders and families
  that map directly onto saleable sub-collections ("UK owls", "UK waders",
  "UK ducks"). Multiplied by even 6 style treatments it exceeds 3,800 SKUs on
  its own.
- Garden birds (~30 species) are the commercially dense subset: high search
  volume, small list. Treat them as a separate premium axis rather than as 30 of
  the 636.
- Butterflies (59) × moths (sample ~40 showy species) is a closed ~100-subject
  axis that can be reproduced in full with binomials — no sampling needed.

### Gaps
- The exact count of **regularly breeding** vs **wintering** British birds was
  not isolated this session. BTO's Bird Atlas gives ~246 regular breeders and
  ~200 regular winterers; this was not re-verified against a BTO page and should
  be checked before being quoted.
- The British Dragonfly Society species count (46 breeding vs 57 recorded) was
  not confirmed on the BDS site itself; figure is from prior knowledge.
- A canonical count of British sea fish (the ~330 figure) could not be sourced to
  a single registry this session.

## WORLD FAUNA THAT SELLS AS WALL ART

### Takeaway
Most world-fauna groups are large but bounded by published global taxonomies
(AviList/IOC for birds, IUCN for mammals, Shark References for chondrichthyans).
The commercially proven groups are small and fully enumerable: 18 penguins,
5 *Panthera* big cats, ~250 owls, ~365 hummingbirds, the "Big Five"/safari set,
and the Arctic set. None carry IP risk; the only caution is that photographic
reference images do.

**IP / risk flags, botanical**

| Item | Risk | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Latin binomials, common names of species | **None** | use freely |
| Cultivar names registered since ~1995 | **Plant Breeders' Rights** (UK PBR, 25–30 yr term for most species, 30 yr for trees/vines) + often a separate **trade-marked selling name** | exclude; use pre-1990 cultivars |
| David Austin rose trade names, "Karma" / "Mystic" dahlia series, "Endless Summer" hydrangea | **Registered trade marks** | exclude |
| RHS, Kew, Woodland Trust, BSBI names and logos | **Trade marks** | never use |
| RHS Plant Finder / Plant Atlas data as a bulk download | **Database right** (UK sui generis, 15 yr) | do not republish the dataset; use it as research only |
| Botanical plates by Redouté (d.1840), Thornton, Curtis's Botanical Magazine pre-1900, Anna Atkins (d.1871), Ernst Haeckel (d.1919), Maria Sibylla Merian (d.1717) | **Public domain** | safe to reference/derive |
| Marianne North, Beatrix Potter fungi watercolours | Potter d.1943 — UK copyright expired 1 Jan 2014; **but "Peter Rabbit" and Potter character marks are live trade marks** | botanical/fungi studies safe; never the characters |

### Inferences
- The botanical axis alone can carry a very large catalogue without touching
  cultivars: 1,692 native British wild plants + 35 native trees + ~70 ferns +
  ~3,000 macro-fungi + ~650 seaweeds ≈ **5,400 IP-free named subjects with
  binomials**.
- Cultivar lists (roses, tulips, dahlias, peonies, apples) multiply this by
  another ~30,000, but each needs a date filter to clear PBR and trade-mark
  risk. A rule of "registered before 1990" is a cheap, safe cut.
- Fungi are the standout under-served domain: 3,000 visually distinctive named
  species, a canonical British checklist, and no IP exposure at all.

- No verified count of **UK native orchid species** (the 52 figure is [T]).
- No verified count of British seaweed species (~650 is [T]).
- The RHS dahlia register size (>20,000) and APS peony register size (~6,500)
  were not verified this session.
- The KAVB registered tulip cultivar count was not verified; only the 15-division
  structure is given, and that too is [T].

### Inferences
- Food and drink has fewer *large* enumerable lists than fauna or places, but
  unusually high SKU density per subject: a single cocktail supports a recipe
  diagram, an exploded-ingredients print, a typographic print and a "cocktail
  wheel". The 102 IBA drinks therefore yield several hundred SKUs.
- The **protected-designation lists (EU eAmbrosia, Defra UK GI)** are an
  under-used canonical source: ~90 UK GIs and ~1,650 EU wine GIs, all named,
  all citable, all free to *depict as places and foods* while being unusable as
  labels.
- The cleanest arithmetic in this domain: 102 cocktails + 34 BJCP categories +
  ~100 BJCP sub-styles + 5 whisky regions + ~55 grape varieties + ~60 wine
  regions + ~30 coffee preparations + ~45 teas + ~200 cheeses + ~45 breads +
  ~70 pasta shapes + ~65 spices + ~50 chillies + ~80 British bakes +
  ~60 gin botanicals ≈ **1,000 named food-and-drink subjects**.

### Gaps
- The BJCP **sub-style** count was not verified; only the 34 categories are
  reliable. Download the guidelines .docx and count.
- The "~750 British cheeses" and "~350 pasta shapes / 1,300 names" figures are
  [T] and unverified.
- The ~1,368 commercial grape varieties figure is [T], from the Anderson/
  Nelgen *Which Winegrape Varieties are Grown Where?* database rather than OIV
  directly; verify before quoting.
- No canonical registry exists for coffee preparations, teas, breads or British
  puddings — these lists are assembled, not harvested.
