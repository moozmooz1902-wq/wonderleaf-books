# European poster and wall-art marketplaces: Displate, Desenio, Posterlounge, Juniqe, Poster Store, Photowall

**Research date: 2026-10-07.** A note on source quality up front, because it materially limits
what can be claimed:

- **Desenio.co.uk, desenio.com and posterstore.co.uk/posterstore.com are not machine-readable
  from this environment.** Every direct fetch returned HTTP 429 with a *Vercel Security
  Checkpoint* bot-protection page (verified by inspecting the response body, not the status code
  alone). Both brands sit behind the same protection, consistent with them being one group. So
  for Desenio and Poster Store I have **search-index snippets of their own pages** (reliable for
  prices and titles that the indexer captured verbatim) but **no full page reads**, and no
  bestseller rank ordering.
- **Displate was fully fetchable** (displate.com and about.displate.com), so Displate findings
  are the best-evidenced in these notes.
- **Posterlounge and Juniqe** were partially reachable via search snippets of their own pages;
  posterlounge.co.uk/p/bestsellers/ 429'd.
- **Photowall: I found no reliable sources in this research pass** and did not get to it before
  hitting my tool budget. See Gaps.
- A large share of what circulates online about "2025/2026 wall art trends" is **print-on-demand
  SEO content** (merchize.com, posterscape.com, inprintdesigns.com, justgreatposters.com,
  faire.com hubs, accio.com). These are content-marketing pages with no disclosed methodology.
  I have kept a few for directional colour but labelled them explicitly as such. **They should
  not be presented as evidence in the final report.**

## Q1. Best-selling and most-promoted categories and subjects (2025-2026)

### Takeaway
Displate's promoted catalogue is overwhelmingly fandom/licensed-IP-led (anime, gaming, film/TV,
cars, space, sport, nature), while the Scandinavian paper-poster players (Desenio, Poster Store,
Juniqe) and Posterlounge are interiors-led: photographic art, botanicals, abstract shapes,
typography/quotes and hand-drawn illustration. None of these companies publishes unit-level
bestseller data; "bestsellers" is a merchandised page, not a disclosed ranking.

### Cited Findings
- Displate's own browse taxonomy / "popular searches" are: **Anime, Movies & Shows, Cars,
  Gaming & Fantasy, Space, Sport, Nature & Landscapes** — [Displate homepage](https://displate.com/)
- Displate's promoted fandoms on the homepage: **Star Wars, Marvel, Cyberpunk 2077, Warhammer,
  Elden Ring, DC Comics, NBA, One Piece, Naruto, Stranger Things, The Witcher**, within **200+
  licensed brands** — [Displate homepage](https://displate.com/)
- Displate's currently promoted *merchandising* units are **Limited Editions, Shards (mini-size
  collectibles) and Custom Displates**, with a sitewide "up to 30% off" code on Matte, Gloss and
  Textra — [Displate homepage](https://displate.com/) (promotion, as of this fetch)
- Displate also maintains dedicated licensed landing pages per IP, e.g. **Magic: The Gathering**
  and **Warhammer** — [Displate MTG](https://displate.com/licensed/magic-the-gathering);
  [Displate Warhammer](https://displate.com/licensed/warhammer)
- Desenio describes its own bestseller page as "a mix of its most popular categories such as

  "a mix of timeless designs and the latest trends" — [Desenio bestsellers (UK)](https://desenio.co.uk/posters-prints/top-list/)
- Named Desenio bestseller items captured by the index: **"Abstract Green Shapes"**, botanical
  **"Olive Branches in Vase"**, minimalist line art, hand-drawn florals, and a **Studio Vreeken**
  artist collaboration — [Desenio bestsellers (UK)](https://desenio.co.uk/posters-prints/top-list/)
- Juniqe UK bestseller titles captured by the index: **"The Swimming Pool", "Sunny Beach",
  "Eat Spaghetti", "Dancing in Paradise", "Bubblegum Lama", "Geese on Vacation", "With Love

  [Posterlounge movie canvas](https://www.posterlounge.co.uk/wall-art/canvas-prints/movies/)

### Inferences
- Juniqe's bestseller titles ("Eat Spaghetti", "Bubblegum Lama", "Do Epic Shit", "Geese on
  Vacation") point to a **playful, illustrative, humour-and-slogan** positioning that is
  distinctly *not* the Desenio minimalist-neutral look, despite the two being commercially
  related. Juniqe reads as the group's gifting/character brand.
- The two aesthetics imply two different generation briefs: Displate rewards **recognisable
  subject matter and fandom specificity**; Desenio/Poster Store reward **restrained, repeatable
  interiors motifs that coordinate with each other**.

### Gaps
- No company publishes ranked unit sales, so "bestseller" on all six sites is a curated
  merchandising page of unknown construction. Treat all bestseller lists as marketing claims.
- I could not retrieve ordered Desenio or Poster Store bestseller lists (bot protection).

## Q2. Desenio and Poster Store: Scandinavian aesthetic, UK strength, bestsellers

### Takeaway
Desenio and Poster Store are **the same company**: Desenio acquired Poster Store in late 2020,
creating a Nordic group with roughly SEK 1-1.3bn of revenue. Both sell cheap, matt, uncoated
paper posters in standard metric sizes designed to be bought several at a time, around a
Scandinavian minimalist/neutral aesthetic. UK strength is evidenced by full localised UK
storefronts (desenio.co.uk, posterstore.co.uk) with GBP pricing, but I found **no hard UK
revenue-share figure**.

### Cited Findings
- **Poster Store was acquired by Desenio on 24 November 2020**; terms undisclosed —
  [CB Insights, Poster Store](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/poster-store)
- The acquisition created a group of **220 employees and over SEK 1 billion in revenue** (legal
  adviser's statement) — [Delphi](https://www.delphi.se/en/news/mergers-acquisitions/delphi-represents-desenio-in-the-acquisition-of-poster-store/).
  A second source puts it at **250 employees and SEK 1.3 billion** —
  [Nordic9](https://nordic9.com/news/desenio-acquires-posterstore-and-expects-the-new-group-to-reach-sales-of-sek-1-billion-news5976860227/).
  These conflict; the SEK 1bn figure is the one in the adviser's own release.
- Pre-acquisition scale: **Desenio SEK 607m (~$70m) in 2019**; **Poster Store founded 2014 by
  siblings Tobias and Oskar Renlund, SEK 90m (~$11m) in 2019**, expecting ~SEK 400m in 2020 —
  [Nordic9](https://nordic9.com/news/desenio-acquires-posterstore-and-expects-the-new-group-to-reach-sales-of-sek-1-billion-news5976860227/)
- Desenio's own self-description: "a webshop selling prints with a **Scandinavian & Nordic
  design**"; "With Scandinavian design as our starting point, our trendy posters fit perfectly
  with diverse interior styles" — [Desenio UK](https://desenio.co.uk/en/)
- Product spec: "printed on **uncoated, premium matt paper**… delivered in a cylindrical tube" —
  [Desenio about our products](https://desenio.co.uk/info/about-our-products/)
- Desenio operates a **UK storefront with GBP pricing from about £7**, online-only, with limited
  personalisation (photo prints) — [98types, best UK poster websites 2026](https://98types.co.uk/blogs/movies/best-poster-websites-uk)
  (third-party UK round-up, not a primary source)
- Desenio Group is a listed entity with a published company description from its share admission
  — [Desenio Group AB company description, 2023](https://deseniogroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/CompanyDescriptionDesenioGroup_en.pdf)
  (PDF retrieved but **image/encoded-stream only; text could not be extracted** in this
  environment — flagged as a gap, and it is the single most promising unexploited source)
- Desenio Group press room — [Cision, Desenio Group](https://news.cision.com/desenio-group/about)

### Inferences
- Desenio's catalogue depth is very large: its size-filtered listing pages paginate to at least
  **page 117** for a single size (30x40 cm) on the US store, which at typical grid sizes implies
  several thousand designs in that size alone — [Desenio 30x40 page 117](https://desenio.com/us/posters-prints/sizes/posters-30x40cm?page=117)
- "Scandinavian" here functions as a **colour and restraint discipline** (off-white/greige
  grounds, black line, muted sage/terracotta, generous negative space, no gloss) more than a
  subject matter — the subjects are generic (botanical, abstract, photographic, typographic) and
  the coherence comes from palette and finish.

### Gaps
- **No UK revenue share or UK-specific bestseller data** for Desenio or Poster Store. The group's
  2023 company description almost certainly contains market-by-market splits; it needs a working
  PDF text extractor (none available here) or a browser read.
- Poster Store's own bestseller page content could not be read at all.
- Whether Juniqe is formally inside Desenio Group: one search result referenced Juniqe "as part
  of the Desenio Group" but **no primary source confirmed an acquisition**. Do not assert this.

## Q3. Displate: dominant subjects, and licensed IP vs independent art

### Takeaway
Displate is a metal-poster marketplace of ~2.5m designs from 40,000+ independent artists plus
200+ officially licensed collections; it has sold **13 million posters** lifetime. The licensed
side is small in catalogue count but is what the company promotes, and its subject mix is
gaming/anime/film/cars/space/sport — i.e. a fandom business, not an interiors business.

### Cited Findings
- **"Over 2.5 million designs"** from **"40,000+ independent artists"**; **200+ licensed
  collections/brands**; **over 5 million fans**; **100-day returns**; Trustpilot "Excellent"
  on **18,628+ reviews** — [Displate homepage](https://displate.com/)
- A Displate is positioned as "a high-quality **metal poster**… a stylish and durable
  alternative to traditional paper and canvas prints", with **tool-free magnet mounting
  included** — [Displate: what's a Displate](https://displate.com/whats-a-displate)
- **13 million posters sold worldwide**, announced alongside "12 years of growth"; an earlier
  milestone of **8 million** is also on record — [Displate stories](https://about.displate.com/our-stories/)
- Production and distribution is **in-house in Łódź, Poland** — [Displate stories](https://about.displate.com/our-stories/)
- Named licensors in Displate's own stories: **Porsche AG, CD Projekt Red (Cyberpunk 2077),
  Netflix (Stranger Things), Lucasfilm**; plus a **Google Cloud / Gemini** technology partnership
  — [Displate stories](https://about.displate.com/our-stories/)
- Licensing history: Displate's rise is dated to **2017, when it signed with The Walt Disney
  Company** for Star Wars and Marvel — [Wikipedia, Displate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Displate)
- **Textra** (3D textured finish) launched **March 2024** — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240327703781/en/Displate-Introduces-Textra-A-Metal-Poster-Bringing-Art-to-Life-in-3D);
  [Displate announcement](https://about.displate.com/our-stories/displate-introduces-textra-a-metal-poster-bringing-art-to-life-in-3d)
- Revenue estimates **conflict sharply**: ECDB puts 2025 sales at **US$99m** with the **US as
  largest market at 32% of revenue** and Furniture & Homeware at 100% of sales —
  [ECDB, Displate](https://ecdb.com/resources/sample-data/retailer/displate);
  Grips Intelligence puts displate.com at **$155.8m in 2025, down 20-50% year on year** —
  [Grips Intelligence](https://gripsintelligence.com/insights/retailers/displate.com)
  Both are third-party estimators, neither is audited; the direction of travel (a decline) is
  only asserted by one of them.

### Inferences
- The **licensed share of catalogue is tiny but the licensed share of promotion is dominant**:
  200+ licensed collections against 2.5m independent-artist designs is a fraction of a percent of
  SKUs, yet licensed fandoms occupy essentially all homepage merchandising. The commercial
  implication is that **independent art on Displate competes for long-tail search traffic, not
  for front-page placement**.
- 13m posters lifetime against ~$99-156m estimated annual revenue and ~$50-70 unit prices implies
  the lifetime total is modest relative to current run-rate — i.e. most of that volume is recent.
- For an independent seller, Displate is **not addressable** anyway: it is a closed marketplace
  where Displate manufactures; it is relevant to this project as a **demand signal for subjects**
  (what fandom/gaming/car/space art sells on metal) rather than as a sales channel.

### Gaps
- **No published split of revenue between licensed and independent art.** The catalogue-count
  ratio is not a revenue ratio and must not be presented as one.
- No buyer demographic data from Displate itself (age, gender, country mix beyond ECDB's 32% US).
- No per-subject sales ranking; the category list is a taxonomy, not a performance ranking.

## Q4. Sizes, formats and price points

### Takeaway
Two distinct price architectures. The Scandinavian paper players sell **metric posters at
£4-£14** with frames sold separately at a multiple of the print price; Displate sells **metal at
roughly $49-$69** in three fixed sizes. Posterlounge spans formats (poster/canvas/acrylic/
aluminium) from under £5 to the low £20s for canvas.

### Cited Findings
**Desenio (UK)**
- Standard sizes include **30x40 cm** and **50x70 cm**, each with its own browse page —
  [Desenio 30x40](https://desenio.co.uk/posters-prints/sizes/posters-30x40cm/);
  [Desenio 50x70](https://desenio.co.uk/posters-prints/sizes/50x70cm/)
- **30x40 cm prints: £6.95 to £21.45**, depending on design — [Desenio 30x40](https://desenio.co.uk/posters-prints/sizes/posters-30x40cm/)
- **50x70 cm prints: from £6.95 to £12.95** — [Desenio 50x70](https://desenio.co.uk/posters-prints/sizes/50x70cm/)
- **30x40 cm frames: £18.70 on sale, £22 full price**; **31 cm poster hangers £16.45** —
  [Desenio 30x40 frames](https://desenio.co.uk/frames/sizes-frames/30x40-frames/)

**Juniqe (UK)**
- Size bands: **S (<30 cm), M (30-49.9 cm), L (50-69.9 cm), XL (70-99.9 cm), XXL (100 cm+)**;
  large-format up to **150x100 cm** — [Juniqe UK wall art](https://www.juniqe.com/uk/wall-art);
  [Juniqe Eye-Catcher large format](https://www.juniqe.com/uk/eye-catcher)
- **Posters from £6.95**; individual bestselling posters **£3.95-£13.95** —
  [Juniqe UK posters](https://www.juniqe.com/uk/wall-art/posters);
  [Juniqe UK bestsellers](https://www.juniqe.com/uk/wall-art/wall-art/bestseller)
- **Acrylic glass prints £9.95-£27.95**, discounted from up to £46.95 —
  [Juniqe acrylic bestsellers](https://www.juniqe.com/uk/wall-art/acrylic-prints/bestseller)
- Juniqe also sells **apparel and gifts** alongside wall art, i.e. it is not a pure wall-art
  retailer — [Juniqe UK apparel](https://www.juniqe.com/uk/apparel)

**Posterlounge (UK)**
- **Posters from £4.87**, typical standard posters **£5.56-£6.95** —
  [Posterlounge posters](https://www.posterlounge.co.uk/wall-art/posters/)
- **Canvas prints £11.01-£23.76**, commonly with ~15% off; **canvas handmade in Germany** —
  [Posterlounge canvas](https://www.posterlounge.co.uk/wall-art/canvas-prints/)
- Unusual aspect ratios are merchandised as their own browse pages, e.g. **20x60 cm panoramic**,
  and **portrait format** as a filter — [Posterlounge 20x60](https://www.posterlounge.co.uk/wall-art/posters/20-x-60-cm/);
  [Posterlounge portrait canvas](https://www.posterlounge.co.uk/wall-art/canvas-prints/portrait-format/)

**Displate**
- Three sizes: **M 45 x 32 cm (17.72 x 12.6 in), L 67.5 x 48 cm (26.60 x 18.90 in), XL 90 x 64 cm
  (35.44 x 25.20 in)** — [Displate FAQ](https://displate.com/about-faq)
- Three finishes **Matte, Gloss, Textra**; **XL is matte only**; **Textra is limited to a few
  hundred designs and to size M** — [Displate FAQ](https://displate.com/about-faq);
  [Displate buying guide](https://www.topbubbleindex.com/blog/displate-buying-guide/) (third-party)
- Indicative pricing **Matte $49, Gloss $59, Textra $69**, varying by size/finish/frame —
  [Displate buying guide](https://www.topbubbleindex.com/blog/displate-buying-guide/)
  (third-party blog, **not** a Displate price list — treat as approximate)
- Displate also sells sub-poster **"Shards" mini collectibles** — [Displate homepage](https://displate.com/)

### Inferences
- The **30x40 cm / 50x70 cm metric pair is the backbone of the European paper-poster market**, and
  Juniqe's S/M/L/XL/XXL banding maps onto the same ladder. A UK-targeted catalogue should be
  authored at ratios that cut cleanly to **30x40 and 50x70 (3:4 and 5:7)** — note these are *not*
  the same ratio, so a single aspect ratio cannot serve both without cropping.
- **Frames carry the margin, prints carry the traffic**: a £6.95 print against a £22 frame of the
  same size means the frame is ~3x the print. A print-only seller is competing in the cheapest,
  most commoditised part of the basket.
- Displate's price is **10x the paper players'** for comparable wall area, which is only
  sustainable because it is sold as licensed fandom merchandise and a collectible, not as decor.

### Gaps
- No verified Displate GBP price list; the $49/$59/$69 figures come from a third-party blog.
- Poster Store's own size/price grid could not be read (bot protection), though as a Desenio
  Group brand it is likely to use the same metric ladder. Do not state this as fact.
- Photowall sizes/prices: not researched (see Gaps at end).

## Q5. Catalogue size and how catalogues are organised

### Takeaway
Catalogue scale differs by two orders of magnitude: Displate ~2.5m designs, Posterlounge
~100k-200k motifs (sources conflict), Desenio in the low thousands per size. Organisation is
consistently **multi-axis**: subject/theme, style, size, format, colour, room, and
collection/gallery-wall — with "bestsellers" and "new in" as merchandised entry points.

### Cited Findings
- **Displate: "over 2.5 million designs", 40,000+ independent artists, 200+ licensed
  collections** — [Displate homepage](https://displate.com/). Organised by **category**
  (Anime, Movies & Shows, Cars, Gaming & Fantasy, Space, Sport, Nature & Landscapes), by
  **fandom/licence**, and by **merchandised range** (Limited Editions, Shards, Custom) —
  [Displate homepage](https://displate.com/)
- **Posterlounge: catalogue figures conflict** — "more than 200,000 motifs", "more than 100,000
  designs", and one estimate of "approximately 81,240 different designs"; **community of 10,000
  artists**; founded **2003**, family-run by founders **Falk Teßmer, Florian Teßmer and Mandy
  Reinmuth** — [Posterlounge about us](https://www.posterlounge.com/about-us/);
  [beyond-print profile](https://www.beyond-print.net/posterlounge-a-neat-splash-of-color-for-everyday-life/)
- Posterlounge revenue estimates also conflict: **US$19m in 2025** (ECDB) vs a **$15-25m** range
  vs **$6.7m** elsewhere — [ECDB, Posterlounge](https://ecdb.com/resources/sample-data/retailer/posterlounge);
  [ZoomInfo](https://www.zoominfo.com/c/posterlounge-gmbh/351053393)
- Posterlounge organises by **format first** (posters, canvas prints), then by **subject**
  (e.g. movies), **orientation** (portrait format) and **exact size** (20x60 cm) —
  [Posterlounge canvas](https://www.posterlounge.co.uk/wall-art/canvas-prints/);
  [Posterlounge 20x60](https://www.posterlounge.co.uk/wall-art/posters/20-x-60-cm/)
- Desenio organises by **category/style, by size** (dedicated 30x40, 50x70 pages), and sells
  **frames and poster hangers as separate size-matched ranges** —
  [Desenio prints](https://desenio.co.uk/posters-prints/);
  [Desenio 30x40 frames](https://desenio.co.uk/frames/sizes-frames/30x40-frames/)
- Juniqe organises by **format** (prints, posters, acrylic), **size band** (S-XXL), **large-format
  "Eye-Catcher"**, and **bestseller/gift collections** — [Juniqe UK wall art](https://www.juniqe.com/uk/wall-art);
  [Juniqe gifts bestsellers](https://www.juniqe.com/uk/c/gifts/gifts/adultbestseller)
- Posterlounge traffic/audience data exists third-party — [SimilarWeb, posterlounge.co.uk](https://www.similarweb.com/website/posterlounge.co.uk/)

### Inferences
- **Every one of these retailers merchandises a "bestsellers" page as a primary navigation entry.**
  That is itself the most actionable observation: the bestseller page is the highest-intent
  surface, and it is curated, which means **what sells is partly what the retailer chose to put
  there** — a feedback loop, not a pure demand signal.
- Posterlounge's conflicting catalogue counts (81k / 100k / 200k) are most likely a mix of
  *motifs* vs *motif-format combinations* vs marketing rounding. Use "over 100,000 motifs" as the
  defensible figure and flag the range.
- Displate's 2.5m designs over 40,000 artists is **~62 designs per artist on average**, which is
  the signature of bulk/series uploading rather than individually crafted pieces. This is directly
  relevant to the near-duplication risk this project tracks.

### Gaps
- Desenio and Poster Store publish no total catalogue count that I could retrieve.
- I could not confirm whether Desenio/Poster Store/Juniqe offer explicit **colour** filters, which
  Desenio is widely believed to; I saw style and size axes only in retrievable pages.

### Takeaway
Very little that is hard. Displate publishes volume milestones and licensing news but no
demographics or category performance. Desenio describes its aesthetic and its bestseller mix in
marketing language only. **Buyer-demographic data for all of these retailers is essentially
absent from public sources**, and the third-party trend statistics in circulation are mostly
unsourced SEO content.

### Cited Findings

  detailed category performance data** — [Displate stories](https://about.displate.com/our-stories/)

  (third-party estimate)
- Desenio's public statement about its own bestsellers is descriptive, not quantitative: "a
  wonderful mix of our most popular categories" / "a mix of timeless designs and the latest
  trends – we have something for all interior design styles" —
  [Desenio bestsellers](https://desenio.co.uk/posters-prints/top-list/)

- *Unsourced SEO claim, flagged as such*: "Gen Z tends to like retro posters, vibrant colours, or
  gaming-inspired decor" — [Merchize](https://merchize.com/wall-art-trends/)

### Inferences
- The absence of published demographics across all six retailers means any audience claim in the
  final report has to be framed as inference from product mix. The defensible version:
  **Displate's mix implies a younger, predominantly male fandom buyer; Desenio/Poster Store's mix
  implies a mainstream interiors buyer skewing female and renting/first-home**; Juniqe's
  slogan-and-character mix implies a **gifting** buyer. These are inferences from catalogue, not
  evidence.

### Gaps
- **No demographic data (age, gender, income, country mix) published by any of the six.**
- No investor-relations deck read for Desenio Group (its 2023 admission document would likely
  carry market splits and order economics, but the PDF was not text-extractable here).
- No 2025-2026 trend report published by any of these companies was located; the trend material
  I found is all third-party content marketing.

## Q7. Gallery-wall / multi-print buying pattern

### Takeaway
The gallery wall is structurally central to how the Scandinavian retailers are built — frames and
hangers are sold as size-matched ranges precisely so customers buy several prints at once — but
the quantified claims circulating about multi-print share of UK purchases come from **unsourced
commercial content and should not be relied on**.

### Cited Findings
- Desenio sells **size-matched frame ranges** and markets them for gallery walls: "You can create
  a gallery wall by mixing and matching several frames"; poster hangers are pitched as "a great
  addition to a gallery wall" — [Desenio 30x40 frames](https://desenio.co.uk/frames/sizes-frames/30x40-frames/)
- Juniqe merchandises a distinct **large-format "Eye-Catcher"** range (up to 150x100 cm), i.e. the
  single-statement-piece alternative to the multi-print wall — [Juniqe Eye-Catcher](https://www.juniqe.com/uk/eye-catcher)
- UK sellers merchandise **gallery wall print sets** as their own product category —
  [Animato gallery walls](https://animato.uk/collections/gallery-walls)
- *Unsourced commercial claims, explicitly flagged — do not treat as evidence*: "gallery-wall and
  ready-to-hang sets now represent roughly **30-35% of residential unit purchases**" in the UK and

### Inferences
- **The strongest evidence for the gallery-wall pattern is architectural, not statistical**: the
  retailers' own catalogue structure (size-matched frames, hangers, size-filtered browse, print
  sets as a category) only makes commercial sense if baskets routinely contain multiple prints.
  That is a sound inference and does not need the fabricated percentages.
- The multi-print pattern has a direct consequence for generated catalogues: **prints must
  coordinate within a palette and style family to be bought in threes**, which is in direct
  tension with the near-duplication risk this project tracks. "Coordinating set" and
  "near-duplicate" are separated by deliberate variation in subject and composition, not by
  recolouring the same image — the distinction matters commercially and for listing policy.

### Gaps
- **No trustworthy quantitative data on prints-per-order, average order value or gallery-wall
  share of baskets for any of these six retailers.** Everything I found was either unsourced
  content marketing or wholesale rather than consumer data. This is the biggest evidential hole
  in these notes and the report should say so rather than cite the 30-35% figure.
