# Generate 150,000 Designs, Not Millions

The decisive finding is that nothing about image generation constrains this project: 2.5 million FLUX.1 schnell images cost roughly **$590–$2,479 of rented GPU time** and **1,736 GPU-hours** ([RunPod pricing](https://www.runpod.io/pricing), [JarvisLabs FLUX benchmark](https://jarvislabs.ai/ai-faqs/best-gpu-for-flux)), while storage for the listing imagery is **about $15/month on Cloudflare R2** ([R2 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/)). What constrains it is eBay. The duplicate-listings policy prohibits more than one fixed-price listing of an item that is not "significantly different", **explicitly including across different usernames**, with account suspension on the enforcement ladder ([eBay UK duplicate listings policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/listing-policies/duplicate-listings-policy?id=4255)) — so the four-account structure is not a workaround, it is four accounts to lose. Monthly selling limits must be *earned* through demonstrated sales ([eBay selling limits](https://www.ebay.ie/help/selling/listings/selling-limits?id=4107)), and a low-sell-through catalogue is precisely the profile that does not earn increases. The correct target is therefore **100,000–200,000 distinct designs collapsed into multi-variation listings** (eBay allows 250 variations per listing at no extra fee, [Trading API variations guide](https://www.developer.ebay.com/api-docs/user-guides/static/trading-user-guide/variations.html)), which still yields 1.8–2.4 million *SKUs* — millions of items, in the sense that matters, from a design count that can actually be made distinctive. On what to generate, the evidence points away from the styles every POD blog recommends and toward four things: **vintage/antique plate restoration with visible texture, British wildlife and British coastal subjects, botanicals led by olive and eucalyptus rather than monstera, and nursery animal trios** — rendered in warm earth neutrals, sage/forest green, and Etsy's declared 2026 colour of the year, **Patina Blue** ([eRank Etsy trends 2026](https://help.erank.com/blog/etsy-trends-2026/)). The hard legal line is short and absolute: no characters or franchises, no logos or brand text, no celebrity likeness, **no royal insignia (a criminal offence under s.99 Trade Marks Act 1994, [Novagraaf](https://www.novagraaf.com/en/insights/royal-wedding-whats-ip-etiquette/))**, and no named artist dead less than 70 years anywhere in a prompt or a listing title.

## Etsy's own data points hard at wall art, which is the opportunity and the warning

The single strongest demand signal in this research is published by the marketplace itself. Etsy's Spring/Summer 2026 Seller Trend Report states that **searches for "wall art decor" are up 110% and "gallery prints" up 80%**, under a named trend it calls *Everyday Exhibits*, and it tells sellers directly to optimise for **"gallery wall set", "vintage wall art"** and **"textured print"** and to build **2- and 3-piece bundles** ([Etsy Insights, S/S 2026](https://community.etsy.com/forum/etsy-success-300/topic/etsy-insights-explore-the-trends-shaping-spring-and-summer-2026-165859/)). eRank's keyword tracking shows "wall art" ranking **#3 overall on Etsy for 2025 and #1 in August 2025** ([eRank, top Etsy searches 2025](https://help.erank.com/blog/top-etsy-searches-in-2025/)). That is the case for building now. It is equally the case for scepticism: Etsy published those numbers to every seller on the platform, and it told them all to make the same 2- and 3-piece vintage gallery sets. Being early to a published trend is not being early, and the 2–3 piece vintage set is the format that will saturate fastest.

What actually moves volume, measured rather than forecast, is narrower than the trend copy suggests. Scraped Etsy sales estimates put the fastest-moving non-custom print listings at **"Flower Market Print, Set of 9" — 12,852 sales at 1,252/month** and **"Printable Vintage Gallery Wall Art SET | Antique Prints" — 13,838 sales, est. $255,996** ([Alura, top-selling Etsy prints](https://www.alura.io/top-selling-etsy-items/prints)). Divide revenue by sales and the pricing lesson is sharper than the subject lesson: those two sets average **~$18.50 and ~$20.00 per order**, against **£4.00** for a single "Wheat Wall Art" printable in the same list. **A cohesive set is worth four to five times a single file, and buyers pay it.** The rest of Etsy's velocity leaders — pet portraits at **40,293 lifetime sales** for one shop, custom name and "first word" keepsake prints, personalised neon signs ([EtsyHunt, top Etsy wall art](https://etsyhunt.com/best-etsy-wall-art)) — are not generatable by an image model at all, because they need per-order customer input. Treat that segment as out of reach rather than as a target.

The other platforms are different businesses wearing the same product. Society6's live bestseller feed is **minimalist landscape painting, abstract organic forms and whimsical animal illustration**, with one artist (Dan Hobday) holding three of the visible slots and a hard **$30–$40 price floor** ([Society6 best-selling art prints](https://society6.com/collections/art-prints-best-selling)) — a curated-taste channel that rewards a recognisable house style over breadth, and one that restricts AI work in its community guidelines. Redbubble is fandom and identity merch printed on paper (music, anime, film franchises, Y2K dorm aesthetics), positioned for "dorms, bedrooms, offices" ([Redbubble best sellers prints](https://www.redbubble.com/shop/redbubble+best+sellers+prints)); it publishes no ranks and blocked automated access to its bestseller feeds with HTTP 403, so there is no item-level Redbubble evidence here at all. Displate is a closed metal-poster marketplace where **licensed fandom occupies essentially all front-page merchandising** — Star Wars, Marvel, Cyberpunk 2077, Warhammer, One Piece, across **200+ licensed brands** ([Displate homepage](https://displate.com/)) — which is useful only as proof that fandom subject matter sells, and fandom is the one thing this pipeline must never generate. INPRNT bans AI-generated work outright. **Etsy and eBay are the addressable channels; the others are demand signals.**

The European paper-poster players define the aesthetic floor instead. Desenio — which **acquired Poster Store in November 2020** to form a group of roughly **220 employees and SEK 1bn+ revenue** ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/poster-store), [Delphi](https://www.delphi.se/en/news/mergers-acquisitions/delphi-represents-desenio-in-the-acquisition-of-poster-store/)) — describes its own bestseller mix as **"photographic art, posters with quotes, hand-drawn illustrations, graphical posters"** and names items like *Abstract Green Shapes* and the botanical *Olive Branches in Vase* ([Desenio bestsellers](https://desenio.co.uk/posters-prints/top-list/)). Juniqe's UK bestseller titles — *The Swimming Pool*, *Eat Spaghetti*, *Bubblegum Lama*, *Geese on Vacation* ([Juniqe UK bestsellers](https://www.juniqe.com/uk/wall-art/wall-art/bestseller)) — read as a gifting and humour brand, not an interiors one. Posterlounge carries **"more than 100,000 motifs"** from 10,000 artists ([Posterlounge about us](https://www.posterlounge.com/about-us/)). Every one of these retailers merchandises a "bestsellers" page as primary navigation, and every one of those pages is curated rather than ranked — so "what sells" on these sites is partly what the retailer chose to put there. None of the six publishes unit sales. That is a real evidential limit on this whole question and it should not be papered over.

### The subject and style list, weighted by evidence

Three sources converge, independently of the POD blogosphere, on the same core: botanical prints, landscapes, **abstract in earth tones (clay, terracotta, khaki)**, nature-inspired art, oversized statement work, **"moss wall art"** and **"vintage artwork"** as named high-potential keywords ([eRank, Etsy trends 2026](https://help.erank.com/blog/etsy-trends-2026/)); vintage botanical prints and wave line art ([Printify, Etsy 2026](https://printify.com/blog/leading-trends-for-etsy-sellers-in-2026/)); and, from the UK trade side, contemporary abstract, botanicals and nature, minimal and monochrome, geometric, and **curated regional and cultural themes — local scenes and cityscapes** ([Trowbridge Gallery, top UK wall art categories](https://www.trowbridgegallery.com/trade-art-insights/top-wall-art-categories-driving-best-sellers-for-uk-stockists-2982)). That last item appears in no US-led trend report, which is exactly what makes it interesting.

On treatment, the 2026 signal is a turn *away* from flat digital minimalism. Etsy's own report names **"textured, handcrafted appearances"** and muted palettes; eRank names **"freehand brushstrokes, raised surfaces, ceramic-like finishes"** and declares **Washed Linen its first Texture of the Year**; the UK trade view asks for **textured and mixed-media pieces** at bold scale. This matters more than it looks: *the styles 2026 buyers want are the styles that hide AI tells*. Impasto, linocut, aged lithograph paper, watercolour granulation and risograph misregistration all carry physical artefacts that read as "texture, story and soul", while flat vector-looking output reads as cheap — and "looks cheap" plus visible pixels is the one concrete buyer complaint pattern documented in the poster market ([Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/funkycurator.com)). Minimalist line art, the most-recommended style in every seller blog and therefore the most supplied, is the one to under-weight. So is boho neutral abstract, which keeps high search volume while reading as 2021.

Four palettes are corroborated across UK and marketplace sources: **warm earth neutrals** (mushroom, cashmere, creamy beige, soft khaki, washed linen); **green** (sage, smoky jade, eucalyptus, olive, deep forest as a "new neutral"); **returning moody blue**, headlined by **Patina Blue as Etsy's 2026 Colour of the Year** ([eRank](https://help.erank.com/blog/etsy-trends-2026/)) and corroborated as a UK interiors direction ([Leekes, 2026 decorating trends](https://www.leekes.co.uk/blog/inspiration/decoration-trends/decorating-trends-2026-spring-summer-2026-interior-design-trends-uk-to-refresh-your-home)); and **terracotta/mustard as accent**, not as a whole palette. Cool grey is described by three independent UK sources as displaced ([Rust-Oleum 2026 colour trends](https://rustoleumcolours.co.uk/paint-colour-trends-for-2026/), [DFS 2026 design trends](https://www.dfs.co.uk/inspiration/2026-design-trends), [Trowbridge](https://www.trowbridgegallery.com/trade-art-insights/current-uk-wall-art-trends-for-2026-influencing-designers-4733)). Patina blue on a washed-linen texture is the most specific, most recent and most citable instruction available, and it comes from the marketplace itself — which also means it is currently under-supplied relative to the demand it will create.

Two negative findings are as useful as the positives. **Sport, aviation, cars/motorcycles and music attract no mention in any 2026 wall-art forecast reached** — multiple sources enumerate ten-plus subjects and none includes them. And **monstera and pampas have no 2025–2026 bestseller evidence at all**; the current botanical evidence points to olive, eucalyptus and wildflower meadow. Do not build a 2019 boho catalogue by inheritance.

## UK buyers shop by colour and room, in metric sizes, and they say "print" not "poster"

The most transferable UK finding is a listing-title grammar, copied from the highest-volume eBay UK wall-art seller observed. Wallfillers, reported at **98.6% positive feedback and 51,000 items sold**, titles its listings in a consistent order — `[Set of N] + [colour 1] [colour 2] + [room] + [subject] + "Canvas Wall Art" + [Print/Pictures] + [size or colour count] + [numeric SKU]`:

> *Set of 3 Teal Canvas Wall Art Prints UK Living Bed Room Pictures 3033*
> *Mustard Yellow and Grey Swirl Bedroom Canvas Wall Art - Abstract Print*
> *London Skyline Canvas Wall Art Print - 17 Colours Available - 94cm wide*

([Wallfillers eBay UK store](https://www.ebay.co.uk/str/wallfillers-canvas)). Three things fall straight out of that. **Colour leads, subject follows** — and Abstract House, at the opposite end of the price range with observed prices of **£115–£774**, independently runs eight colour collections (Blue, Brown, Green, Black & White, Neutral Palette, Red, Vibrant Colours, Yellow) as primary navigation ([Abstract House](https://www.abstracthouse.com/)). Two UK sellers at opposite price extremes both treating colour as the primary axis is the strongest cross-source signal in this research about how UK buyers shop prints: they are matching a room scheme, not collecting an artist. **The room name goes in the title** — "Living Room", "Bedroom" — which is free to replicate and which the trend reports entirely ignore. And **"Print" and "Pictures" are used, never "Poster"**, which agrees with the one keyword finding worth carrying: "wall print" shows **UK at 37% of global volume against the USA's 20%** ([RankHero, "wall print"](https://www.rankhero.com/keywords/wall-print)) — a weak source, but two weak signals agreeing on terminology is more useful than either alone.

Critically, Wallfillers sells **one design in 17 colourways inside a single listing**. That is the resolution to the tension between the colour-led market and this project's near-duplication risk: colourway variation is how the UK market works, but the proven seller puts the variants *inside one listing*, not across many. Duplication must be measured at the **listing** level, and colourway must be a variation attribute, never a new SKU-as-listing.

The UK size ladder is metric and square-friendly, not the American inch ladder. Abstract House runs **50×50, 50×70, 70×70, 70×100, 100×100 cm**; Desenio's core pair is **30×40 and 50×70 cm at £6.95–£12.95** with **30×40 frames at £18.70–£22** ([Desenio 30×40](https://desenio.co.uk/posters-prints/sizes/posters-30x40cm/), [Desenio frames](https://desenio.co.uk/frames/sizes-frames/30x40-frames/)); Juniqe bands S to XXL up to 150×100 cm ([Juniqe UK wall art](https://www.juniqe.com/uk/wall-art)); UK trade guidance recommends **at least one piece at 120×80 cm or larger** for primary spaces ([Trowbridge](https://www.trowbridgegallery.com/trade-art-insights/current-uk-wall-art-trends-for-2026-influencing-designers-4733)). Note that 30×40 (3:4) and 50×70 (5:7) are *not* the same ratio, and neither is the A-series 1:1.41 this catalogue already renders to — A-series scales free across A5 to A0, which is why **A4 and A3 are described as the gift-buying volume sizes and A3+ the margin sizes** ([PrintShrimp, what sizes to sell](https://printshrimp.com/blogs/news/what-sizes-to-sell)). The existing size contract (A4 2480×3507, A3 3508×4961, A2 4961×7015 at 300 dpi) is the right spine; generate at A-ratio and supply 3:4 and 5:7 crops as secondary files rather than re-cropping per order. One frame observation worth acting on: Desenio's **£22 frame against a £6.95 print** means frames carry the margin and prints carry the traffic, and Houzz UK measured **"oak kitchen" searches up 214%** ([Interior Daily / Houzz UK](https://www.interiordaily.com/article/9742034/houzz-reveals-key-uk-interior-design-trends-for-2025/)) — oak frame mockups will likely outperform black for 2026.

Price positioning is a three-tier UK ladder with a visible gap in the middle. eBay UK mass-market canvas sits lowest (no GBP price could be captured — eBay UK returned HTTP 403 to every automated request, which is the largest evidential hole in this research). Fy!, London-founded with **25,001 art prints**, prices at **£19.95–£46.95** ([Fy! art prints](https://iamfy.co/collections/art-prints)), and Etsy UK personalised prints transact at **£8.99–£56 with near-universal 20–55% discounting** ([Etsy UK personalised print](https://www.etsy.com/uk/market/personalised_print)). King & McGaw's licensed museum reproductions run **£75–£230**, editions **£260–£600** ([King & McGaw](https://www.kingandmcgaw.com/prints)). **A UK A3 physical offer around £18–£25 sits in the gap between Etsy's £2–£8 digital commodity floor and Society6's $30 ceiling** — and the heavy discounting norm means list prices should be set with an anchor-and-discount presentation in mind, or the listing will read as expensive next to its neighbours.

Two UK specifics must be designed around rather than discovered later. **Olive et Oriel is Australian, not a UK comparator** — it prices in AUD from **AUD $69.95**, produces in NSW, and has no GBP storefront ([Olive et Oriel](https://www.oliveetoriel.com/)); the brief's premise there is wrong. And **UK Mothering Sunday is a moveable Lent date in March, not the US May date** ([Time and Date](https://timeanddate.com/holidays/uk/mothering-sunday)), against UK Mother's Day retail spend projected at **£1.57bn** ([Statista](https://statista.com/statistics/1287462/mothers-day-spending-united-kingdom)). The other anchor is Christmas, where **decoration articles were 10% of most-desired UK Christmas gifts in 2025** ([Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1084794/christmas-gifts-desired-by-uk-consumers/)) and search interest for wall art posters peaked in **November** ([RankHero](https://www.rankhero.com/keywords/wall-art-poster)). Because eBay ranking needs weeks of sales history, **listings must be live by early October**. Today is 7 October 2026: Christmas 2026 is effectively gone for anything not already uploaded, and the real target is Mother's Day in March 2027 with a full-catalogue build over the winter.

### The UK opportunity that does not fit the technology

The best-evidenced UK-specific subject is place: Desenio runs a seven-city UK map range (London, Leeds, Edinburgh, Manchester, Liverpool, Glasgow, Newcastle) in Scandinavian black-and-white ([Desenio maps and cities](https://desenio.co.uk/posters-prints/maps-and-cities/)); whole businesses exist on nothing else, including **Pubstops, which maps the pubs of over 100 UK towns in Underground-map style** ([Pubstops](https://pubstops.co.uk/)); the highest price observed on any Etsy UK personalised print was **£56 for a Travel Adventures Map Print**; and each town's geometry genuinely differs, so the axis scales without near-duplication. It is also the subject diffusion models are worst at. **Legible place names, accurate street geometry and chart labels are precisely where FLUX.1 schnell fails**, and the same applies to the mushroom identification charts and anatomical plates that sell in the vintage segment. The honest resolution is a split pipeline: generate the imagery, composite the text and geometry as vector layers afterwards. The existing typography renderer already draws clean type at the size contract — **it is the asset that unlocks the best UK subject, and the image model is the wrong tool for it on its own.**

## The high-volume sellers win on sales per listing, not on listing count

The clearest competitive evidence in this research is a direct comparison of two opposite Etsy strategies. **NorthPrints: 253,396 lifetime sales, 8,658/month, from 1,042 listings.** **sofiamastery: 176,029 sales, 5,218/month, from 5,138 listings.** **retirementfund: 195,358 sales from 259 listings.** ([Alura, top Etsy prints shops](https://www.alura.io/top-etsy-sellers/prints)). NorthPrints earns roughly **eight sales per listing per month**; sofiamastery earns roughly **one**. The tight catalogue is eight times better per unit of work, and the difference is not restyling — the top listings differentiate by *subject* across a systematically numbered series, visible in the trailing internal SKUs (`S9-3`, `#252`) in their titles. That is exactly the shape a generated catalogue takes, which is encouraging; what it is not is a licence to add listings.

The physical marketplaces show the same ceiling from the other side. **Displate's own About page says "over 1.5 million" designs**, while its homepage currently claims **"over 2.5 million"** from **40,000+ artists** ([Displate About Us](https://displate.com/about-us), [Displate homepage](https://displate.com/)) — the figures are inconsistent across the company's own surfaces, so treat 1.5M as the defensible number and note that either way it is **37 to 62 designs per artist, the signature of bulk series uploading**. Against that catalogue Displate has sold **13 million posters lifetime** ([Displate stories](https://about.displate.com/our-stories/)) at estimated 2025 revenue of **$99m (ECDB) or $155.8m (Grips Intelligence)** — two third-party estimates that disagree by more than 50% ([ECDB](https://ecdb.com/resources/sample-data/retailer/displate), [Grips](https://gripsintelligence.com/insights/retailers/displate.com)). Per design per year that is a few dollars at best, and Displate concentrates demand on a curated front page and licensed IP in a way eBay does not. **Displate is not a precedent for eBay listing economics; it is a precedent for how little the marginal bulk-uploaded design earns.**

Every large catalogue is organised on the same multi-axis grid, and this is directly reusable as a prompt and taxonomy matrix: **subject × style × colour × size × format × room × collection**, with "bestsellers" and "new in" as merchandised entry points. Fy!'s taxonomy is the cleanest validated instance — nine styles (Maximalist, Cottage Core, Modern, Scandinavian, Art Deco, Bohemian, Abstract, Industrial, Coastal), seven rooms (Living Room, Bedroom, Home Office, Kitchen, Hallway, Bathroom, Kids' Room) and eight subjects (Music, Vintage, Film, Flowers, Animals, Travel, Food & Drink, Botanical) ([Fy!](https://iamfy.co/collections/art-prints)). Note the room order: living room and bedroom first, and they are the only two rooms Wallfillers names in eBay titles. **Kitchen and bathroom are structurally weak for paper prints** — moisture means sealed or metal substrates ([Trowbridge](https://www.trowbridgegallery.com/trade-art-insights/current-uk-wall-art-trends-for-2026-influencing-designers-4733)) — and the Houzz demand measured in those rooms is for fittings, not art.

Sets are the other universal structure, and the only one with a price mechanism attached. eBay UK has a dedicated **"Set Of Prints" browse node** ([eBay UK](https://www.ebay.co.uk/b/bn_7023600434)); Abstract House has *Set of Three Prints* and *Gallery Walls & Sets*; Desenio runs a **"Perfect Pair"** category and curated poster packs ([Desenio perfect pair](https://desenio.com/g/gallery-walls/perfect-pair/)); Olive et Oriel has *Sets & Pairs*. The Etsy arithmetic already quoted — ~$18.50–$20 average order on sets against £4 on a single — is the quantified version. The subjects that set naturally are those with an enumerable series: **species trios, moon phases, triptych landscapes, matched abstract variations, nursery animal trios, and three-stage sequences** (sunrise/noon/dusk; the same tree across seasons). The discipline is absolute: a set is intentional close variation sold as **one listing**. If set members leak out as separate listings, that is the 89% near-duplication failure reproduced exactly.

## The ceilings are eBay policy, selling limits and sell-through, in that order

The arithmetic of this project is lopsided, and worth stating plainly before the risks.

| Constraint | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Generation, 2.5M images | 1,736 GPU-hr; **$590** on a $0.34/hr community RTX 4090, **$2,479** on an A100 | [RunPod](https://www.runpod.io/pricing), [JarvisLabs](https://jarvislabs.ai/ai-faqs/best-gpu-for-flux) |
| Generation, 150k images | ~104 GPU-hr; **~$35**; 4.3 days on one GPU, under a day on five | derived from the same |
| Hosted alternative | **$3,500** (Fireworks, $0.0014/image) to **$7,500** (fal.ai, $0.003/MP) for 2.5M | [Fireworks](https://fireworks.ai/blog/flux-launch), [fal.ai](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/flux/schnell) |
| Listing imagery storage, 2.5M | ~1 TB, **$15/month**; six images each, **$90/month** | [R2 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/) |
| Pre-rendered print files, 2.5M | **~112 TB, $1,680/month** — for files with a <0.1% hit rate | same |
| Masters + render on demand | 5 TB, **$75/month** | same |
| Four eBay Anchor shops | **£437/month each = £20,976/year ex VAT** | [eBay UK shop fees](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/store-fees?id=4809) |
| Same volume on Featured tier | **£31,175/month per account** at 625k listings | derived from the same fee table |

Two conclusions follow immediately. **Anchor is mandatory, not optional** — it is roughly 71× cheaper than the next tier down at this volume, and unlimited zero-insertion-fee listings apply only to fixed-price, so auction-style is structurally unavailable (which also closes the duplicate policy's auction exception as a route). And **print files must be rendered on demand**, storing only the prompt/model/seed recipe plus a compressed master; pre-rendering costs 22× more for a file set that will almost never be printed. Against the £20,976/year of subscriptions, at an assumed ~£8 contribution per sale, the catalogue must produce about **2,622 sales/year — one sale per 954 listings per year** across 2.5M listings. That is a low bar, and it is the honest case for the strategy. The risk was never the height of the bar.

The risk is policy. eBay's duplicate-listings rule is a bright line: more than one fixed-price listing of an identical item from the same seller is prohibited, **including listing in different categories or using different usernames**, and listings that **"aren't significantly different"** count as duplicates, with enforcement running from removal through warning and restriction to **account suspension** ([eBay UK duplicate listings policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/listing-policies/duplicate-listings-policy?id=4255)). Two genuinely different generated designs are not duplicates however similar they look, so the exposure is specifically (a) near-identical generations from one prompt-seed family and (b) the separate **search and browse manipulation policy**, which bans keyword tactics that could mislead buyers and requires every word in a listing to refer only to the item for sale ([eBay UK policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/listing-policies/search-browse-manipulation-policy?id=4243)). A room-and-colour keyword matrix stamped across a design family is exactly what that policy describes. Enforcement is substantially complaint-driven, which makes competitor reporting a live risk at scale.

Selling limits are the harder practical ceiling. eBay caps items an account may list or sell per calendar month, sets limits by account and category, **counts Good 'Til Cancelled listings against the limit, and will not auto-renew GTC listings once it is reached**, reviewing limits monthly against actual sales volume and feedback ([eBay selling limits](https://www.ebay.ie/help/selling/listings/selling-limits?id=4107)). Reaching 625,000 active listings on one account requires eBay to have granted a monthly limit above 625,000 — through repeated reviews driven by sales. A low-sell-through catalogue does not earn those increases. **The business model contains a feedback loop that works against its own scale target**, and that loop is tightened by the reported eBay position that **listings with no sales after 90 days may harm search relevance at account level** ([Frooition, 2022](https://www.frooition.com/blog/2022/05/are-slow-selling-items-damaging-your-ebay-search-relevance/) — trade reporting of a verbal statement, not an official policy page). If account sell-through feeds Best Match, adding hundreds of thousands of non-converting listings dilutes the ranking of the listings that do convert. The returns curve is plausibly not flattening but turning negative.

**No published source gives the listing count at which eBay sales per listing turns down.** Any specific threshold in a plan would be invented. The only honest instrumentation is the seller's own: track **sales per 1,000 active listings per account, monthly**, and stop adding when that ratio falls faster than listings are added. The four existing accounts are better evidence than anything available publicly.

On wall-clock, the reframing is the most useful single number in this report. At 2.5 s/image, 2.5M images is **72 days on one GPU** — needing ~10 GPUs for a week or ~72 for a day. At 150,000 designs it is **four days on one GPU, under a day on five**, which removes scheduling as a consideration entirely and leaves the whole budget for quality: more steps where they help, generous over-generation with automated rejection, and real upscaling. That last item is a requirement rather than a refinement. Schnell's native 1024–1536 px must reach **A2 at 4961×7015** under the existing size contract, and UK demand is drifting to 100×100 cm and 120×80 cm; the one documented buyer complaint pattern in this market is visible pixels at print size. **300 dpi at the largest size sold is the floor, and a dedicated upscaling stage is part of the pipeline, not an afterthought.**

Two internal figures deserve correction against the notes. The brief describes a **2.3M-listing** typography catalogue; the repo's own plan output records **2,784,923 listings before the ink and palette rework and 2,500,000 after**, across four buckets (`luxvia-art` 1,000,000, `mercury-usm` 500,000, `lunar-kms` 500,000, `posterleaf-store1` 500,000). And **95% of that catalogue is slot-filled personalisation** — names, towns, breeds crossed with a handful of templates — while the generic niches a buyer finds *without* typing their own name are 37 to 200 phrases stretched 20 to 68 ways. The image catalogue is the fix for the second problem, not an extension of the first. It should be sized to that job.

## The legal line is short, absolute, and mostly about what the image contains

The law moved decisively in this seller's favour. In **Getty Images v Stability AI [2025] EWHC 2863 (Ch), 4 November 2025**, the High Court held that **model weights are not an "infringing copy" because they do not store copies of training images** ([Browne Jacobson](https://www.brownejacobson.com/insights/getty-images-stability-ai-judgment-uk-copyright-law-analysis), [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/uk/stability-ai-defeats-getty-images-copyright-claims-in-first-of-its-kind-dispute-before-the-high-cour)). Getty abandoned its training claim after conceding no evidence training occurred in the UK, and its only win was narrow and on **trade marks** — iStock and Getty watermarks appearing in outputs ([Browne Jacobson](https://www.brownejacobson.com/insights/getty-images-stability-ai-judgment-uk-copyright-law-analysis)). For a downstream UK seller, training-data litigation is close to a non-risk; an appeal is expected during 2026 ([Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/ai-and-copyright-uk-outlook-for-2026)).

The transferable lesson from Getty's one victory is the cheapest and highest-value control available: **reject any generated image containing legible text, signatures, watermark-like marks or logo-like forms.** That single automated gate is both a copyright and a trade-mark control, it has direct judicial support, and it costs almost nothing because AI text is usually garbled anyway. It is also the gate that makes the vintage-plate segment safe — generate the plate, composite the Latin binomial afterwards.

The exclusion list is short enough to encode as a hard filter:

| Subject | Right engaged | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Film/TV/game/comic characters, anime, franchises | Copyright + trade mark | **Exclude absolutely** — the top VeRO trigger |
| Film posters, game key art, album covers | Copyright | **Exclude** |
| Logos, brand names, football club crests, brand typography | Trade mark (TMA 1994) | **Exclude**; reject incidental legible brand text |
| Watermarks and stock-agency marks in output | Trade mark — *Getty v Stability* | **Exclude** — a proven infringement route |
| Celebrity likeness | Passing off (*Irvine*; *Fenty v Arcadia*), plus copyright in source photos | **Exclude** |
| Royal likeness, Royal Arms, crowns, royal emblems | **Criminal offence, s.99 TMA 1994**; copyright in official photographs; Trade Descriptions Act 1968 | **Exclude absolutely** |
| Living or recently-dead named artists (Picasso d. 1973, Dalí d. 1989, Warhol d. 1987, Rothko d. 1970, Hopper d. 1967) | Copyright in specific works; trade mark in names; passing off | **Exclude** — never in prompt or metadata |

Royal subjects need a harder rule than the rest because **s.99 of the Trade Marks Act 1994 makes it a criminal offence** to use the Royal Arms or deceptively similar designs so as to suggest authorisation, and the Trade Descriptions Act 1968 adds fines and possible imprisonment for false indications of royal approval ([Novagraaf](https://www.novagraaf.com/en/insights/royal-wedding-whats-ip-etiquette/), [Osborne Clarke](https://marketinglaw.osborneclarke.com/marketing-techniques/royal-wedding-warning-for-advertisers/)). Criminal exposure is not insurable and not appealable the way a takedown is. On celebrity likeness, the UK's lack of a codified image right is a narrow advantage, not a licence: **the Court of Appeal in *Fenty v Arcadia* confirmed that an unauthorised Rihanna image on a t-shirt amounted to passing off** ([RPC](https://www.rpclegal.com/thinking/ip/ip-alert-court-of-appeal-confirms-rihannas-image-protected/)) — and wall art sold under a shop name is the same fact pattern. Football is a licensing trap worth naming explicitly: UK clubs enforce crests, kits and stadium imagery aggressively, and eBay's VeRO programme is the route they use.

**Style itself is not protected by copyright anywhere** ([Mayer Brown / JSM](https://www.jsm.com/publications/2025/05/drawing-the-line-copyright-issues-in-ai-generated-style-imitation)), and UK copyright runs **life of the author plus 70 years** ([Bridgeman Images](https://www.bridgemanimages.com/en/news/what-is-copyright-and-when-does-it-expire/16802)). So generating in the manner of an artist who died before roughly 1956 — Van Gogh, Monet, Hokusai, Klimt, Mucha, Turner, ukiyo-e, Victorian botanical and ornithological plates, period maps and patent drawings — is genuinely safe, doubly so because both the style and the underlying works are clear. *THJ Systems Ltd v Sheridan* (Court of Appeal, November 2023) strengthens this further: **a faithful photograph of a two-dimensional public-domain artwork attracts no new copyright** ([Creative Commons](https://creativecommons.org/2024/01/18/uk-court-clears-path-for-open-culture-to-flourish/), [Museums Association](https://www.museumsassociation.org/museums-journal/opinion/2024/02/how-does-a-recent-landmark-ruling-change-museums-understanding-of-copyright/)), so museum scans are not laundered back into copyright by the institution that photographed them. The traps are contractual rather than copyright: a hi-res file downloaded under terms forbidding commercial use creates breach-of-contract exposure regardless, Crown copyright runs on a different 50-year clock, and trade marks never expire. The operational rule is sharper than the doctrine: **the distinction that matters is prompt versus metadata.** Naming an artist in a prompt is private; naming one in a title, tag or description is a public act that creates discoverability, supports a false-endorsement argument, and hands a VeRO reporter everything they need. Describe the aesthetic generically — "1920s Art Deco geometric", "impressionist harbour scene", "mid-century abstract colour field".

VeRO itself is notice-and-takedown, not adjudication: a rights owner reports, eBay removes, and the seller's recourse is an internal appeal or persuading the reporter to retract, with consequences escalating to **account suspension** ([eBay UK VeRO policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/listing-policies/verified-rights-owner-vero-program?id=4349)). **A seller cannot rely on being legally right** — a defensible listing can still be removed, and the removal still counts. Reading VeRO participant profiles for the wall-art category before bulk upload is a cheap pre-flight step that eBay itself recommends.

Three further points close the legal picture. First, **the "schnell only" rule in CLAUDE.md is correct but the stated reasoning is wrong.** FLUX.1 [dev]'s *model* is non-commercial and needs a paid Black Forest Labs self-hosted commercial licence, but its model card states the *outputs* may be used commercially ([Hugging Face, FLUX.1-dev](https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.1-dev), [artificialguy on FLUX licensing](https://artificialguy.com/blog/flux-licensing-commercial-use/)). The operative risk is running dev in a revenue-generating pipeline, not selling a dev-made poster. Schnell's Apache 2.0 remains the right operational bright line; the strongest alternative is **Qwen-Image (20B), Apache-2.0 — but Qwen-Image-2.1 is under a research licence and must not be used** ([Hugging Face Qwen-Image](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Image), [apidog](https://apidog.com/blog/what-is-qwen-image-2-1/)). Pin the version. Avoid SD 3.5 and SDXL-Turbo, whose Community Licence silently converts to requiring an Enterprise Licence above **$1M annual revenue from any source** ([stability.ai/license](https://stability.ai/license)). Second, **assume the catalogue has no enforceable copyright.** CDPA 1988 s.9(3) assigns authorship of computer-generated works to whoever made "the arrangements necessary", but most consultation respondents supported its removal and the government has signalled it may go ([HSF Kramer, March 2026](https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/ip/2026-03/uk-government-report-on-copyright-and-ai-concludes-more-evidence-is-needed-although-s-9-3-cdpa-could-go)), while *THJ*'s originality test — free and creative choices stamping the work with a personal touch — is hard to satisfy for model output. Do not build a business that depends on stopping others copying. Third, **eBay does not currently require AI disclosure**; the binding rule is that the image must represent the actual item sold, which is straightforward when the AI image *is* the product. Voluntary plain-language disclosure in the description is still worth doing: it reduces misrepresentation exposure under UK consumer law and pre-positions for **EU AI Act Article 50 obligations arriving August 2026** if anything is sold cross-border. Note the contrast with Etsy, which requires labelling by degree of human involvement under its Creativity Standards ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/09/etsy-new-seller-policy-2024-generative-ai)).

## What to generate, at what scale, in what order

Pulling the evidence into a build plan. The recommended shape is **~150,000 distinct designs** — roughly 6,250 subject atoms × 6 style treatments × 4 palettes — published as **~150,000 multi-variation listings** carrying 12 SKUs each (A4/A3/A2 × unframed/black/white/oak), which is **1.8M SKUs** inside the 250-variation cap and within reach of a single week's GPU time. Phase it so that the first 12,000 designs are the ones with the best evidence and the lowest AI risk, and so sell-through data arrives before the catalogue is committed.

| Block | Subject atoms | Why, and the evidence | AI risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vintage/antique plate restoration | 800 | Highest-velocity real Etsy sets are antique/vintage gallery sets; Etsy and eRank both flag texture; aged paper hides AI tells | Low — **but composite all labels as vector, never generate them** |
| Botanical | 1,200 | In every 2026 forecast. Olive, eucalyptus, wildflower, fig, lemon; UK-native cow parsley, foxglove, bluebell, dog rose. **Not monstera or pampas** | Very low |
| British wildlife and birds | 400 | A settled six-species expectation (hare, fox, badger, hedgehog, stag, pheasant) sold as a set at £14; highland cow has its own Etsy UK markets and 250+ reviews on one print, and spills into cushions | Low in watercolour and line art |
| UK coastal and countryside | 900 | Coastal is a named category at Fy!, Abstract House and Olive et Oriel; pairs naturally with the returning patina/maritime blue; Cornish coves, Lake District fells, Yorkshire dales, drystone walls, harbour boats, lighthouses | Low |
| Textured and earth-tone abstraction | 1,000 | Abstract is second in every demand ranking; impasto and brushstroke are the 2026 treatment; eBay UK's leading canvas seller sells abstract swirls by colour pair | Very low — the safest high-volume block |
| Nursery | 600 | Highest claimed category volume; the format is fixed at sets of 3 and 5; the subject sets are standardised (safari lion/elephant/giraffe, woodland bear/fox/deer); plus a **UK farm set** (sheep and lamb, highland calf, duck, pony) that US catalogues lack | Lowest of all — no hands, text or perspective. **Highest duplication risk** — differentiate by palette and treatment |
| Celestial, still life, food and drink, architecture | 1,050 | Secondary but evidenced; Desenio's coffee poster pack is a live anchor | Medium for architecture (perspective) |
| UK places and plans | 500 | Best-evidenced UK subject and the highest-priced personalised format, but **text and geometry led** | **High — hybrid pipeline only** |

Styles, in evidence order: **textured/impasto brushwork; vintage lithograph and antique plate; watercolour (wet-edge, granulating); minimal single-weight line art; fine-art photographic look (grain, muted grade, shallow depth of field); linocut/block print**. Add risograph and Japandi as experiments rather than commitments — no 2025–2026 marketplace source names riso as a selling style. Palettes, as reusable prompt fragments: warm neutral/washed linen; green (sage to deep forest); **patina and inky blue, over-weighted for 2026**; terracotta/mustard as accent inside the other three. Add **blush pink** as a fifth on the strength of Houzz UK's measured >100% growth in "pink kitchen" and "pink bathroom" searches plus Wallfillers' *Blush Pink Grey Black* bestseller title. And design **grey as the secondary, warm as the primary** — the trend press has declared grey over while the actual eBay UK bestseller titles are full of it.

Hard rules for the pipeline, each traceable to a specific finding above. Colourways and sizes are **variations inside one listing**, never separate listings. Sets are **one listing**, and set members must never leak out individually. Titles follow the eBay UK grammar — colour, room, subject, "Wall Art Print", size, SKU — using **"Print" and "Pictures", not "Poster"**, and nothing in a title may be a keyword that does not describe the item. Every output passes a text-and-mark rejection gate before it reaches a listing. No artist dead less than 70 years appears in any prompt or any field. Generate at A-ratio, upscale to 300 dpi at the largest size sold, store recipes plus compressed masters and render print files on order. Instrument sales per 1,000 active listings per account monthly, and let that number, not a target, decide when to stop adding.

Two existing repo decisions should be revisited in light of this. The **142,904 harvested museum scans** are legally clean and commercially poor — faded mezzotints on yellowed paper, which is not what a UK eBay buyer is searching for; the photometric curation filter already written (`pd_quality.py`) targets 15,000–20,000 survivors from 153,178, and that is the right call. Generated vintage-plate work in the same idiom will outperform the scans, because it can be made crisp, palette-controlled and A-ratio native from the start. And the **four buckets still have empty `pic_base` values in `plan.json`**, with R2 credentials present only for `tshirt-m12k`; nothing uploads until that is resolved, and the same R2 signing limitation that blocks Claude from holding those keys applies here. The RunPod template pattern that worked for t-shirts (credentials in the template, inherited by the pod) is the route.

## Conclusion

The most counter-intuitive result of this research is that the project's scale ambition and its commercial logic point in opposite directions, and the evidence sides with the smaller number. Two Etsy shops in the same category differ eightfold in sales per listing on the strength of catalogue discipline alone, Displate's bulk-uploaded long tail earns single-digit dollars per design per year, and eBay's own machinery — selling limits that must be earned through sales, plus an account-level sell-through signal — actively penalises the strategy of adding listings faster than they convert. Four Anchor shops cost £20,976 a year whether they hold 150,000 listings or 2.5 million. The 150,000-design catalogue reaches millions of items through variations, costs about £27 of GPU time, finishes in days rather than months, and leaves the entire quality budget available for the upscaling and texture work that separates a £20 print from a £2 bundle file. The million-design catalogue buys nothing the variation mechanism does not already provide, and buys real policy exposure.

The second result worth carrying forward is that the best UK opportunity and the chosen technology do not match, and saying so is more useful than papering over it. Place is the strongest UK-specific subject in this research — seven-city ranges at Desenio, standalone businesses covering 100-plus towns, the highest price observed on any personalised Etsy UK print — and it is text-and-geometry work that diffusion models render badly. Meanwhile the subjects schnell renders beautifully (botanical, abstract, coastal, watercolour animals) are the most supplied and most duplication-prone in the market. The resolution is already half-built in this repo: a clean typography renderer that draws type to an asserted size contract, sitting next to an image pipeline that cannot. **Composite, don't generate, anything with words in it** — and the differentiation problem, the vintage-plate opportunity, the UK place opportunity and the legal text-and-watermark gate all resolve through the same architectural decision.
