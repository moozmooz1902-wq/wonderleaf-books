# Eleven axes multiply into millions of designs

Five live wall-art catalogues were enumerated to exhaustion through their public Shopify endpoints — **6,918 collections across Fy!, Society6, Olive et Oriel, The Poster Club and Abstract House** ([iamfy.co/collections.json](https://iamfy.co/collections.json?limit=250&page=1), [society6.com/collections.json](https://society6.com/collections.json?limit=250&page=1)) — and they resolve into **eleven organising axes carrying roughly 1,850 distinct enumerated values**, of which the richest single source is Fy!, whose entire catalogue is itself axis-generated from 671 base tokens crossed with a five-value medium axis and a 36-value wall-position axis nobody else in the market has. The arithmetic that matters is this: the **~8,550 hard-enumerable UK subjects** available from official registries (636 BOU birds, 221 Royal Kennel Club dog breeds, 3,495 BSBI plants, 282 Munros, 88 IAU constellations, 102 IBA cocktails and so on), crossed with the **139 style and technique tokens that carry no copyright surface at all**, yield **1.19 million designs before a single palette or format variant is applied**, and ~3.6 million at three palettes each — which lands the catalogue between Displate's self-stated 1.5 million designs ([Displate About Us](https://displate.com/about-us)) and the 2.5 million on its own homepage, with Juniqe's stated **318,775 designs** and Photowall's **20,366 motifs** as the curated-retailer floor. The generation side is constrained by two facts most FLUX advice gets wrong: schnell's T5 window is **256 tokens, not 512**, and **negative prompts do nothing** at `guidance_scale=0`, so every unwanted element — above all legible text, signatures and logo forms — must be handled by positive description, deterministic cropping and an OCR gate rather than negation ([FLUX.1-schnell model card](https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.1-schnell), [diffusers FluxPipeline docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/en/api/pipelines/flux)). The near-duplicate index is the keystone: one HNSW index over CLIP embeddings serves simultaneously as the dedup gate, the space-filling cell selector and the Vendi-score measurement, which is the direct answer to the 89%-near-duplication failure recorded on the wall-art branch. One honest limit frames everything below: **nine of the surveyed sites refuse automated access and not one site on the open web published a numeric review or sales count**, so "weight by what sells" rests on Photowall's item counts, Shopify `products_count` ordering, navigation prominence and cross-retailer repetition — not on sales data.

## Eleven axes, 1,850 values, and the one axis only Fy! has

The harvest converges on a stable axis model. Every retailer reached organises wall art on some subset of the same eleven axes, and Fy! names seven of them itself through its own `art-by-*` collections — `art-by-artist`, `art-by-colour`, `art-by-gift`, `art-by-mood`, `art-by-room`, `art-by-style`, `art-by-subject` — while *generating* two more (medium and wall position) as SEO-only crosses with no navigation entry at all. That gap between what a retailer navigates and what it generates is the single most reusable structural finding in the corpus.

Two further axes exist but are not reproducible by generation and are recorded only so they can be deliberately declined: **artist / named IP** (dominant at The Poster Club with ~40 of 111 collections, and at Olive et Oriel with ~38) and **provenance** (Abstract House's `original-art`, `limited-edition-prints`, `sold-original-paintings`, plus its price bands — the only price-banded original-art taxonomy found). Four axes are unique to one host and worth noting as competitive white space: Olive et Oriel's **wall surface** (wallpaper, murals, decals, mirrors, height charts), its **geo-SEO doorway grid** (`kids-wallpaper-in-{us-city}` across 12 cities), its **child age/gender segmentation** (`girls-0-5yrs`, `teen-boys-wallpaper`), and The Poster Club's **three-dimensional wall objects**.

The axis values that are *physically* implausible are as instructive as the sensible ones. Fy!'s generator has bled: `bathroom-above-bed`, `kitchen-disco`, `childrens-room-above-toilet`, `living-room-above-bed` and `home-office-hallway` are all live collections. Any generated taxonomy needs a validity constraint table, because a generator left unconstrained produces exactly this.

## Retailers cross only two axis pairs, at 18% and 27% density

Fy! is the only retailer whose cross-density can be measured, because its handles expose the grammar. The result is the most commercially useful number in the harvest. If every content axis were crossed — 137 subject × 98 style × 151 mood × 6 medium × 11 room × 36 position × 35 colour × 35 occasion × 160 persona × 11 format — the theoretical space is **1.04 × 10¹⁶ collections**. Fy! publishes about **1,690**. The fill rate is roughly **one in 10¹²**. Its taxonomy is overwhelmingly *additive*: axes laid side by side, with only two pairs genuinely multiplied.

| Cross Fy! actually makes | Cells | Filled | Density |
|---|---|---|---|
| base token × medium/format | 671 × 6 = 4,026 | 712 | **17.7%** |
| room × wall position | 11 × 36 = 396 | 107 | **27.0%** |
| set size × room | 2 × 7 = 14 | 14 | **100%** (but Dining Room, Nursery and Playroom are excluded from the cross entirely) |
| persona × anything | 160 | 160 flat | **0%** — completely uncrossed |
| artist/IP × anything, place × anything, colour × medium | — | flat | **~0%** |

This is the evidence about what is worth making. **Mood × medium is the densest cross Fy! runs** — roughly 120 mood bases carry three to six mediums each — followed by subject × medium, where the high-value subjects (`animals`, `birds`, `dogs`, `flowers`, `landscapes`, `places`, `people`, `travel`, `insects`, `dinosaurs`, `trees-and-forests`, `ocean-and-marine`, `wild-animals`) all carry four or five. The eight bases carrying *all six* medium/format values are `abstract`, `beliefs`, `bohemian`, `bold`, `cool`, `culture`, `neutral`, `scandinavian`. By contrast `baroque`, `bauhaus`, `victorian`, `expressionism`, `futurism`, `rustic`, `grandmillennial` and `art-nouveau` carry **no** medium cross at all, and the occasion axis is crossed with exactly one medium — watercolour — for fourteen of its values. Room is never crossed with medium; rooms cross with position instead. Per-medium reach ranks **photography 181 bases > illustration 158 > watercolor 123 > drawing 107 > canvas 88 > typography 55**, and typography is applied only to text-bearing or mood bases, never to `birds` or `dogs`.

Photowall supplies the only hard distribution data recovered anywhere, and it is blunt: of **20,366 motifs**, Nature (9,864) and Art & Design (8,379) are **~90% of the catalogue**; Landscape orientation is 12,074 of 20,366 (**59%**); Illustrated outnumbers Photographic **13,713 to 6,560**; Living Room + Kids Room + Bedroom are 5,271 of roughly 8,160 room-tagged items; and within style, Vintage (1,399) is more than three times Minimalist (390) and eight times Japandi (169) ([photowall.co.uk/posters](https://www.photowall.co.uk/posters)). Society6's grid gives the same shape from the other side: **679 of its 1,099 themes (62%) exist on exactly one product type**, and only **24 themes are crossed onto ten or more** — `70s`, `abstract`, `animals`, `anime`, `black-and-white`, `boho`, `botanical`, `cat`, `color`, `colorful`, `digital`, `floral`, `funky`, `funny`, `gothic`, `japanese`, `matisse`, `mid-century-modern`, `modern`, `pattern`, `preppy`, `retro`, `vintage`, `william-morris`. Those 24 are the closest thing in this research to a validated demand list.

Shopify's `products_count` ordering adds a per-collection depth signal, with a caveat: the field is internally inconsistent (Fy! reports 802,338 for `cooking-art-prints` against 607,517 for the whole `art-prints` catch-all; Olive et Oriel reports identical 10,592 for `most-popular`, `least-popular` and `modern-wall-art`; The Poster Club reports zero for thirty-plus live collections). It is usable only for relative ordering within one host, and for detecting duplicate smart-collection rules. On that basis Fy!'s deepest *taxonomic* collections run Portrait (551,504), Birthday (242,260), Nature (215,789), Vibrant (211,533), New Home (177,675), Housewarming (167,004), Warm (162,682), Cool (159,193), Earthy (158,169), Abstract (155,109), Bohemian (152,336), Muted (140,883), Bold (134,025), Monochrome (128,313), Calming (122,202). **Mood and occasion out-rank style in depth**, which is the opposite of where most POD catalogues put their effort.

### The route from axes to 1.19 million designs and 3.6 million SKUs

The real retailers' catalogue sizes set the benchmark band. Curated European operators run **20,366 motifs (Photowall)**, **40,000+ (Poster Store)**, **100,000+ motifs (Posterlounge, with 81k / 100k / 200k all in circulation)** and **318,775 designs (Juniqe, stated on its own wall-art listing page)**. The two generative/UGC marketplaces run **1.5 million designs on Displate's About page against "over 2.5 million" on its homepage** ([Displate About Us](https://displate.com/about-us), [Displate](https://displate.com/)), and Society6 reports **809,068 products in `abstract` alone** across 32 product types. A multi-million design catalogue is therefore at the Displate end of a precedented range, not off the scale.

The arithmetic that gets there does not come from crossing the retail axes with each other — that is the 10¹⁶ fantasy — but from crossing the **enumerable subject registries** against the **copyright-free style and technique axis**:

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Hard-enumerable UK-first subject pool | 815 British fauna + 266 dog and cat breeds + ~130 native livestock breeds + 4,727 botanical (1,692 native plants, 35 trees, 3,000 visible fungi) + 817 UK places + 335 celestial + 136 cocktails and beer styles + 127 symbol sets + 196 weather/time + 1,000 dinosaur genera | **~8,550 subjects** |
| Extended pool | plus 10,000+ tartans, ~70,847 RHS plant names, 1,368 grape varieties, 2,500+ moths | **~93,000 subjects** |
| × copyright-free style axis | 83 technique tokens + 30 interior labels + 26 fully-cleared movements | **139 style tokens** |
| Subject × style | 8,550 × 139 | **1,188,450 designs** |
| × palette variants (applied as post-process, see pipeline) | × 3 | **~3.57 million designs** |
| Format renders | A-ratio and 5:7 share one render with differential crop; 3:4 needs its own | **~7.1 million renders** |
| eBay SKUs across four accounts | designs as listings, with size and substrate as listing *variations* | **~3.6 million listings, ~890k per account** |

Three things discipline that arithmetic. First, **palette is nearly free** because the only method that reliably forces a 3–5 colour result on schnell is nearest-colour mapping in CIELAB after generation — which means one render can be emitted as several genuinely different catalogue entries, and the palette axis becomes exactly controllable rather than approximately steered. Second, **size and substrate must stay inside the listing, not across listings**: Saatchi Art advertises "Available in 5 sizes, 4 materials" on a single listing, Wallfillers sells one design in 17 colourways in one listing, and that is the third independent retailer in this corpus putting the size/material matrix inside one product. Third, **gallery sets must be rule-limited, not combinatorial** — C(1000,2) is 499,500 pairs from a thousand designs, and the whole market stops at trios, with Fy!'s Living Room Gallery Wall at 21,843 products being the deepest set collection found. Sets should be generated from curated style-and-palette families, with Dining Room, Nursery and Playroom filled in since Fy! leaves all three empty.

The two axes to expand on, where a new entrant adds breadth without adding near-duplicates, are precisely the ones Fy! leaves **flat**: persona (160 values, zero crosses) and place (8 values, zero crosses). Crossing the 160-persona axis against the UK place gazetteer and the enumerable subject registries is open ground.

## A hundred and thirty-nine style tokens carry no copyright surface at all

The style axis divides into three sub-axes that combine multiplicatively — **movement is a draughtsmanship axis, technique is a surface axis, and interior label is a palette-and-texture axis** — and museum glossaries already treat movement and technique as orthogonal tags on one item, with MoMA's index running "Abstract Expressionism / Abstraction / Acrylic paint / Action painting" in a single flat list ([MoMA collection terms](https://www.moma.org/collection/terms/), [Tate art terms](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms)). The total enumerated vocabulary is **218 tokens**: 60 movements, 40 printmaking and photomechanical techniques, 28 drawing and painting media, 15 documentary/scientific/cartographic traditions, 30 interior labels, 30 pigment names and 15 named palette systems.

**The 83 technique tokens and 30 interior labels carry zero copyright exposure**, because a process name cannot be owned and there is no artist death year to check. They are also the highest-variance axis available: the same wren rendered as mezzotint, risograph, cyanotype and sumi-e gives four genuinely different images, whereas the same wren across four Impressionist painters gives four near-duplicates. This is the structural answer to near-duplication, and it argues for weighting technique far above movement in the generation plan. Representative prompt-usable characteristics, as harvested: **linocut** — bold simplified shapes, rounded gouge terminations, slightly ragged edge, flat opaque ink, white line as carved mark; **mezzotint** — deep velvet blacks, smooth tonal transitions with no line, luminous form emerging from darkness; **risograph** — one to three spot fluorescent inks, visible misregistration, coarse grain and banding, paper show-through; **cyanotype** — Prussian-blue monochrome, white silhouette where masked, uneven brushed emulsion edges; **chromolithograph** — 8–16 flat colour stones, stippled colour overlay creating secondary hues, saturated Victorian palette, slightly off registration; **wood engraving** — end-grain fine white line on black, dense cross-hatch, silvery grey from massed line; **sumi-e** — single-stroke forms, dry-brush split hairs, abundant empty ground, graded ink.

Under the UK term — 70 years from the end of the year of the author's death ([CDPA 1988 s.12](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/12)) — the clean cut in 2026 is **died 1955 or earlier**. Twenty-six movements clear it on every principal artist checked, four are partial, and six unlock on **1 January 2027**.

There is a crucial operational distinction the legal research makes and the market confirms. **Style is not protected by copyright anywhere** — it protects expression, not manner ([Mayer Brown / JSM on AI style imitation](https://www.jsm.com/publications/2025/05/drawing-the-line-copyright-issues-in-ai-generated-style-imitation)) — so the cut above is risk management against two specific hazards: outputs that reproduce a recognisable individual work, and marketplace takedowns triggered by artist names in titles and tags. **Using a name in a prompt is private; using it in listing metadata is public, discoverable, and supports a false-endorsement argument.** Fy! and Juniqe both show the right construction: Fy! sells "**William Morris Style**" and "**Art Nouveau / Klimt**" — note the word *Style* and the slash — and Juniqe's named-artist landing pages are **all public-domain artists** (Van Gogh d.1890, Kandinsky d.1944, Klimt d.1918, Hokusai d.1849, Kahlo d.1954) plus one obscure one (Colin Campbell Cooper d.1937). Bimago's 21-painter "Reproductions" range is effectively a ready-made public-domain prompt brief, every name on it out of copyright: Canaletto, Caravaggio, Caspar David Friedrich, Monet, Degas, El Greco, Klimt, Bosch, van Eyck, Vermeer, Leonardo, Michelangelo, Raphael, Rembrandt, Botticelli, Renoir, Titian, Van Gogh, Kandinsky, Blake, Turner ([bimago.co.uk](https://www.bimago.co.uk/)).

On the interior-label sub-axis, the 2026 vocabulary polarises into a calm cluster (Japandi — "the most searched AI interior design style in 2026" per [Spacely.ai](https://www.spacely.ai/blog/luxury-architecture-trends-for-2026) — quiet luxury, wabi-sabi, organic modern, Scandinavian) and a saturated cluster (moody maximalism, dark academia, snug/book nook, hotel chic), and both poles are evidenced, so the catalogue should carry both. The palette story for 2026 is unusually coherent: **Pantone Cloud Dancer 11-4201** ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-news/pantone-names-2026-color-year-rcna247366)), **Etsy's Patina Blue** plus its first-ever **Texture of the Year, Washed Linen** ([Etsy news release](https://www.etsy.com/news/etsy-announces-its-2026-color-of-the-year-a-plus-the-first-ever-texture-of-the-year)), and **Dulux's Rhythm of Blues** three-shade family all point the same way. ## schnell reads 256 tokens, ignores negation, and must be gated rather than instructed

The pipeline specification follows from four hard facts about FLUX.1 schnell and one about the economics.

**Model settings.** schnell is a 12-billion-parameter rectified-flow transformer under **Apache-2.0**, and its model card's own call is `guidance_scale=0.0, num_inference_steps=4, max_sequence_length=256` ([FLUX.1-schnell model card](https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.1-schnell)). The diffusers documentation states `max_sequence_length` "cannot be more than 256" for schnell — **half of FLUX.1-dev's 512, which is what nearly all published FLUX prompting advice assumes** — and that `prompt` routes to CLIP-L (hard 77 tokens, pooled to one global vector) while `prompt_2` routes to T5-XXL ([diffusers FluxPipeline docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/en/api/pipelines/flux)). The practical prompt budget is therefore **~180–200 English words**, and it must be enforced in code by tokenising with `google/t5-v1_1-xxl` at assembly time and rejecting anything over 240 tokens. Because the CLIP path is pooled and feeds the modulation path, it acts as the global style and colour steer: **put the style axis and the palette axis in the first 40–50 tokens**, and put subject detail and composition later. The documented `prompt`/`prompt_2` split — short style-and-palette string to CLIP, full composed prompt to T5 — is the cheapest available lever for making adjacent taxonomy cells look different, and it is untested, so treat it as an experiment. Scheduler is `FlowMatchEulerDiscreteScheduler`, the one diffusers ships; the SD-era sampler zoo does not transfer to a distilled rectified-flow model. The 4→2 step decision is the single largest cost lever in the whole pipeline and deserves a proper per-style A/B — 2,000 cells at 1, 2 and 4 steps, scored with an aesthetic predictor plus 200 human ratings — because flat graphic styles plausibly tolerate 2 steps where watercolour granulation and impasto do not. Going above 4 steps has no support: the published claims that 8–12 steps helps come from mutually contradictory SEO sources and should not be acted on.

**Negative prompts do not work.** The pipeline accepts `negative_prompt`, but negation "is ignored when `guidance_scale ≤ 1`", and schnell requires 0 ([diffusers FluxPipeline docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/en/api/pipelines/flux)). Suppression must therefore be positive-affirmative, deterministic, or by rejection. In practice: describe the ground as a pigmented surface rather than an absence ("on an unbroken field of cold-pressed cotton rag paper, bare warm off-white, the paper tone continuing evenly to every edge" rather than "white background", which is widely reported to make FLUX output blurry); say affirmatively where the picture stops and then crop the overscan, never writing "no border" because the token *border* is a request; kill shadows by naming a medium that physically cannot cast one — ink on paper, flat screenprint layers — so the shadow is out of distribution rather than negated; and **never name a context of display**, because "poster", "print", "wall art", "framed", "gallery wall", "hanging", "mockup" and "room" all pull the model toward photographing a framed print on a wall. The same logic bans style tokens that carry framing as part of their visual identity: "antique botanical plate" and "vintage lithograph" co-occur in training data with page edges, plate borders, captions, foxing and plate numbers, so name the technique and the ink behaviour, not the artefact. Generate with **6–8% overscan on each axis and centre-crop deterministically** — this removes simulated paper edges, vignettes and corner signatures for free.

**Exact dimensions.** FLUX requires both dimensions to be multiples of 16, is trained around 1 MP, and degrades above roughly 1.5 MP — not by graceful softening but by composition duplication and loss of global coherence, because schnell's flow-match scheduler uses a fixed shift where dev's scales with sequence length. Generate at ~1.0–1.1 MP and upscale; there is no case for 2 MP direct generation, since a ~5x upscale is required either way.

| Target ratio | Recommended generation size | Exactness | MP |
|---|---|---|---|
| A-series 1:√2 (A4/A3/A2) | **848 × 1200** | +0.06% vs √2 — within the printer's own trim tolerance | 1.02 |
| A-series, higher option | 1008 × 1424 | −0.11% | 1.44 |
| 3:4 (30×40 cm) | **864 × 1152** | **exactly 3:4** | 0.995 |
| 5:7 (50×70 cm) | **880 × 1232** | **exactly 5:7** | 1.08 |

These are three genuinely different ratios (1.4142, 1.3333, 1.4000), so format is a real generation axis rather than a crop of one master — **except** that A-ratio and 5:7 differ by only 1%, so a single A-ratio render with 2% overscan serves both by differential cropping. 3:4 cannot be served that way.

**Upscaling chain to A2 at 300 dpi (4961 × 7016 px).** From 848 × 1200 the true factor is **5.85× linear**, ~34× in pixels. The chain is two-stage and mostly non-diffusion: a **4× DAT or HAT transformer upscaler** (prefer the Nomos8k-trained DAT/HAT variants over 4x-UltraSharp, which is trained for photographic sharpness and plasticises painterly texture) taking 848×1200 → 3392×4800, then **Lanczos** for the final 1.46× to 4961×7016, which is visually benign and essentially free on CPU. This never holds a 35-megapixel tensor on the GPU. **Reject SUPIR for the bulk run**: the one quantitative contrast available is 4x-UltraSharp at 3–5 s/image against SUPIR at ~45 s/image, and SUPIR's documented tendency to "take creative liberties" is actively harmful when the generated design *is* the product. Keep SUPIR or Ultimate SD Upscale with ControlNet Tile (denoise 0.4, CN weight 0.8) as a hand-finishing path for the small subset that actually sells. The single biggest technical risk in the print pipeline is that GAN upscalers read watercolour granulation as compression artefact and remove it; that needs a dedicated pilot — 200 watercolour and 200 impasto generations through DAT, HAT, Real-ESRGAN, UltraSharp and Lanczos, ten printed at A2 and looked at. Output spec: **JPEG quality 95 at 4:4:4 chroma subsampling** (never 4:2:0 — it destroys the saturated hard edges that screenprint and risograph styles are made of), **8-bit sRGB with the ICC profile embedded**, **300 dpi written explicitly** into the JFIF density and EXIF resolution fields. Prodigi wants RGB at 300 dpi, accepts JPG/PNG/PDF for wall art, and carries 3 mm bleed on photo prints ([Prodigi Images FAQ](https://www.prodigi.com/faq/images/), [Prodigi: create print-on-demand wall art](https://www.prodigi.com/blog/create-print-on-demand-wall-art/)); **do not convert to CMYK** and do not ship 16-bit. PNG at 35 MP is 40–90 MB a file, which is 40–90 TB per million designs against ~10 TB for JPEG.

**Throughput and cost, with an explicit warning.** The published figures are poor. The most usable are **~1.8 s/image on an RTX 4090** (≈33/minute, ≈47,000/day) and **~1.0 s/image on an H100 SXM**, both from low-reliability sources that state neither batch size, resolution, step count nor precision; and jarvislabs.ai gives 6–10 seconds for the same workload without naming a GPU. As a planning figure only, at 4 steps, 1 MP, bf16, batch 4, `torch.compile`, expect **0.5–0.8 img/s on a 4090 and 1.2–2.0 on an H100**, giving roughly **350–550 GPU-hours per million images** on 4090-class hardware — so **~420–660 GPU-hours for the 1.19 million-design core**. Quantisation: **FP8 on Ada or newer is the right choice** (TensorRT gives 1.72× for INT8 and 1.95× for FP8 over `torch.compile` FP16 on RTX 6000 Ada, [NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/tensorrt-accelerates-stable-diffusion-nearly-2x-faster-with-8-bit-post-training-quantization)); **INT8 and NF4 are the wrong choice**, because they are often *slower* than FP16 due to dequantisation overhead and exist for VRAM reduction, not speed. Four-bit costs real fidelity — FLUX.1-dev measures **LPIPS 0.11 with MXFP8 against 0.44 with NVFP4** ([PyTorch blog](https://pytorch.org/blog/faster-diffusion-on-blackwell-mxfp8-and-nvfp4-with-diffusers-and-torchao/)) — and 0.44 is not acceptable when the image is the product. Serve with **raw diffusers in a batched loop in one long-lived process**, text encoders and transformer resident, `torch.compile` applied once at startup, static batch shapes (one per aspect ratio, which is a real argument for grouping work by format), VAE decode and JPEG encode on worker threads. ComfyUI's API is the wrong tool for a batch job; vLLM-style continuous batching does not apply, because diffusion at fixed 4 steps is uniform-length work. The real bottlenecks are T5 encoding (cache it — the taxonomy reuses prompt fragments heavily), 35 MP VAE decode and object-store PUT latency. **Benchmark your own configuration before planning on any of these numbers**; it is half a day of work and will be more reliable than anything published.

**Job structure.** The work unit is a **shard, not an image**. Pre-compute the entire render list — every selected cell with its seed, prompt, dimensions and output key — into an immutable Parquet manifest of ~5,000 rows per shard, written to R2 *before* any GPU starts. Derive the seed deterministically as `blake2b(cell_id) mod 2**31`, so any image can be regenerated exactly and "checkpointing" reduces to listing which output keys exist; no GPU state needs saving, which is what makes spot eviction cheap. Hand out shards with a lease and timeout from a tiny coordinator. Resume by listing the shard's output prefix. Never let a pod idle: exit 0 when no shards remain, make the entrypoint `python worker.py; runpodctl stop pod $RUNPOD_POD_ID`, and run a watchdog *outside* RunPod that terminates any pod whose completion counter has not advanced in 30 minutes — that is the failure mode that actually costs money. Set an account spending cap as backstop. Run generation on spot 4090s or L40S and put upscaling, palette mapping, crop and encode on CPU instances, not on a rented H100.

**Automated quality gating — a cascade, cheapest first.** Stage 0 is pure statistics at 5–20 ms CPU on 100% of images, and the strongest gate is one almost nobody else can run: **palette conformance, as mean and 95th-percentile ΔE2000 from each pixel to its nearest palette entry**, which works precisely because the taxonomy specifies the palette and therefore supplies ground truth. Alongside it: edge-band variance and mean on the outer 2% ring per edge (catches simulated paper edges, vignettes, frames, letterboxing); corner high-frequency energy (catches signatures, which diffusion models put in corners); Laplacian variance plus the radially-averaged FFT power-spectrum slope (the slope is the right detector for soft mush and for the >1.5 MP degradation mode); colour-histogram entropy and distinct-colour count (validates "exactly three flat spot inks"); mean saturation and clipping. Stage 1 is **PaddleOCR in detection-only mode** on 100% of images at 30–100 ms — detection only, because you need to know glyph-like regions exist, not read them — **rejecting on total detected text area above ~0.2% of the image or any detection above ~0.6 confidence**. Tesseract is worse on exactly the stylised, garbled text you need to catch; EasyOCR is accurate but heavy. This gate doubles as the text-form watermark detector. Stage 2 runs on survivors at 5–15 ms batched: the **LAION aesthetic predictor** (CLIP ViT-L/14 + MLP head), where the published tiers are 4.5+/6.5+ for the LAION-Aesthetics dataset and 5.0/6.0 for pre-training/fine-tuning retention ([arXiv 2601.09896](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09896v4), [HiDream-I1, arXiv 2505.22705](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.22705)) — **gate at ~5.5 and rank by score rather than hard-gating at 6.5, and calibrate per style**, because the predictor encodes a specific photographic gaze and will systematically under-score the flat graphic styles (linocut, single-weight line art, cyanotype) the taxonomy most wants. Also at Stage 2: CLIP or SigLIP cosine similarity between the image embedding and the text embedding of the *subject clause only*, with a per-subject threshold set from the distribution, which gates "the model rendered the wrong animal". **Embed once and reuse the vector for three jobs** — aesthetic score, prompt adherence, dedup. Stage 4, VLM judging, goes on a 0.5–1% audit sample and on the shortlist actually being listed; never in the per-image path, because at £0.001 an image it is £1,000 per million. One clarification the report should carry: the "AI slop detector" tools identify content *as* AI-generated, which is the opposite of what is needed here — all the images are AI, and they need ranking by quality.

**Near-duplicate detection.** The published benchmarks are unambiguous that embeddings beat pHash and SSIM by very large margins: pHash manages **0.12 Recall@1** on scene duplicates against DINOv2, and at a fixed review budget of k=5,000, embedding methods recover **76.5% recall against 17.6% for pHash and 35.3% for SSIM** ([arXiv 2312.07273](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.07273v2), [CleanPatrick, arXiv 2505.11034](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.11034)). **SSIM has no place at this scale.** The pipeline is two tiers. Tier 1 is 64-bit pHash with Hamming distance ≤ 4 — 8 bytes per image, 40 MB for 5 million, ~3 ms CPU — catching only re-renders and genuinely identical output. Tier 2 is **CLIP ViT-L/14 768-d embeddings in a FAISS `IndexHNSWFlat` with `M=32`, `efConstruction=200`, `efSearch=128`**; HNSW over IVF-PQ because these are high-recall threshold queries near cosine 1.0, where PQ quantisation error is comparable to the margin being measured. Storage is **15.4 GB for 5 million float32 vectors** plus ~1–2 GB graph overhead, so ~18 GB fits in RAM on one ordinary machine, or ~8 GB in float16. Build time is 5–20 minutes. Thresholds: **cosine ≥ 0.95 is "same design", reject outright; 0.93–0.95 is the lower cited CLIP/DINO boundary and should also reject for a commercial catalogue**, since a false reject costs one more render and a false accept is the documented 89%-near-duplication failure. The **0.85–0.93 band is where the value is, and no published threshold exists for it** — it must be calibrated by hand-labelling 300–500 pairs sampled across 0.80–0.98 against the question "would a buyer see these as the same print?", and reported as calibrated rather than as a literature value. Two cautions: do not assume DINOv2 beats CLIP (the same benchmark found DINOv2 **0.1–0.3 AUC worse** than DINOv1 for near-duplicate detection), and remember that CLIP is semantically driven, so a wren in two different styles may sit closer than two compositions in one style — which is backwards when style is the differentiating axis. The fix is a **composite distance**: reject only when images are close on *both* CLIP cosine and a separate low-dimensional style signature (palette histogram and edge-orientation histogram, already computed at Stage 0). Run dedup **online as a gate, not as a post-hoc audit**, or you will have paid to upscale and store millions of duplicates before finding out.

**Axis sampling — which cells to render.** No published work applies covering arrays, Latin hypercubes or Sobol sequences to image-prompt selection; the recommendation below is a synthesis whose components are cited and whose combination is not. **Stage A: a pairwise (t=2) covering array over the taxonomy**, on CPU, before any render. The justification is the projection insight — designs space-filling in two-dimensional projections are the right target, because adjacent-cell similarity is a pairwise phenomenon ([arXiv 2203.06334](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.06334)). For 400 subjects × 30 styles × 40 palettes × 6 compositions × 3 formats, full enumeration is 8.64 million cells but a pairwise array is on the order of **16,000 rows** — a 540× reduction with a guarantee that every pair of axis values co-occurs at least once. Use NIST ACTS, Microsoft `pict` or `allpairspy`, all of which accept **constraints** for forbidden combinations (cyanotype × a four-colour palette; impasto × "flat shadowless"). Pairwise is the guaranteed *core*, not the answer. **Stage B: expand with a scrambled Sobol sequence** over the orderable axes (palette hue rotation, crop tightness, subject scale, colour count, line weight) via `scipy.stats.qmc.Sobol(d, scramble=True)` — low-discrepancy and, crucially, **incremental**, so the first 100k points today extend to 500k later and the union stays well-spread. Latin hypercube is the wrong tool here precisely because it is fixed-N and cannot be extended. **Stage C: render a 50k pilot, embed, and measure which axes are real.** For each axis, compute the mean pairwise embedding distance between cells differing *only* in that axis; an axis whose single-variable swap moves the embedding less than the within-cell seed variation **is not a real axis** and must be merged or redefined. Expect several palette variants and several "style" nouns to fail. **This is exactly the measurement that would have caught the 89% near-duplication before 424k listings went up.** **Stage D: Poisson-disk selection in embedding space, which is identical to the dedup gate** — accept a new design iff its nearest neighbour in the HNSW index is farther than radius r, with r set from the calibrated threshold. One index, one radius, serving both dedup and space-filling selection: the single most important engineering simplification available. Farthest-point greedy (k-center) is the batch equivalent at O(Nk) with a 2-approximation guarantee; reserve **k-DPP** with kernel `L_ij = q_i · S_ij · q_j` (aesthetic score on the diagonal, similarity off it) for the small high-value selection of which few hundred designs to promote, because greedy MAP-DPP is O(Nk³) and k³ at a million is not a computation. Report **Vendi Score** on the selected set ([arXiv 2210.02410](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.02410)): it is reference-free, which suits a catalogue with no reference, and it reads as an effective number of distinct items. A set of 100,000 designs scoring 2,000 is telling you that you have 2,000 designs and 98,000 copies — the single honest answer to "is this catalogue distinct", and directly comparable to the 89% figure.

It should be non-overridable.

## Nine retailers refuse robots, and not one publishes a sales number

This must not be papered over, because it limits exactly the part of the brief that asked for weighting by what sells.

**Sites that refuse automated access, confirmed against a real Chromium browser with the agent proxy's CA installed, not merely against HTTP fetches:** Redbubble (Cloudflare managed challenge that does not clear, on every path including `/robots.txt`), Etsy (403 with an empty body on every path, including `/c/`, `/market/`, the Seller Handbook and the help centre), Desenio and Poster Store (Vercel Security Checkpoint, HTTP 429 on .co.uk, .com, .eu, .ca and .com.au alike), Posterlounge (429 on .co.uk and .de), King & McGaw (403, Cloudflare), Surface View (403, Cloudflare), the Art Institute of Chicago (403, Cloudflare) and the MoMA Design Store (Cloudflare). Saatchi Art and Juniqe were failing for *technical* reasons — a 403 and a client-rendered SPA respectively — and both became readable in a real browser, which is how their filter facets were recovered. **The distinction matters: the nine above are unavailable in this environment, not merely unsearched.** Going further would mean defeating anti-bot protection, which was not attempted. TeePublic and Zazzle are likewise 403 on every path tried, and Pictowall returned HTTP 522 with its origin down.

**The Wayback Machine is unreachable from here** — `web.archive.org` is refused outright by WebFetch and reset by the proxy on curl, though the `archive.org/wayback/available` JSON API does respond — so there is no fallback for the blocked sites. Current snapshots do exist and are named: a Desenio capture at `web.archive.org/web/20261004211227/https://desenio.co.uk/` (4 October 2026) and a Poster Store capture at `web.archive.org/web/20260614013818/https://posterstore.co.uk/` (14 June 2026). **These are the single highest-value follow-up action and they need a machine that can reach web.archive.org.** Desenio's and Poster Store's complete motif/style/colour/room menus — the two retailers the brief singled out as the four-axis exemplars — remain unharvested, and everything recorded for them comes from marketing copy and live search-result URLs rather than their menus.

**No site exposed a numeric popularity signal of any kind.** No review counts, no star ratings, no favourite counts, no sold counts, for any individual wall-art item on any of the fourteen sites reached. Displate renders only two badge strings, "New" and "Bestselling", in its markup, and a fetch confirmed "no star ratings or review counts are visible". Juniqe and Fy! render such data client-side or not at all. Society6's reviews live on a separate page with no per-item numbers. **So the "weight by what sells" element of the brief cannot be satisfied from these sources, and no sales figures have been invented to fill the gap.** What is available instead, and what the weighting in this report actually rests on, is four proxies: Photowall's per-facet item counts (the only hard distribution data recovered anywhere, 20,366 motifs with counts by motif, colour, room, style, format and image type); Shopify `products_count` as a relative-ordering signal within one host, with its internal inconsistencies documented; navigation prominence and the existence of a dedicated collection; and repetition of the same term across independent retailers. Society6's 24 themes crossed onto ten or more product types, and Fy!'s per-medium base counts, are the nearest thing to a validated demand ranking in the corpus.

Four further gaps should be named plainly. **No first-hand tag harvest exists from any site** — Redbubble is blocked, Society6's Shopify rebuild exposes no public tag index across 2.3 MB of rendered HTML, and INPRNT's robots.txt disallows `/search/*` where tag queries resolve while its sitemap serves 29 `<sitemap>` entries with empty `<loc>` elements. The ~60 tags recorded come from one seller-tooling blog and one search summary. **Etsy's real Style, Colour, Room and Occasion enums were never recovered**; the clean route is not scraping but one authenticated `getPropertiesByTaxonomyId` call for the Wall Decor and Prints taxonomy ids with an Etsy app key. **eBay's authoritative aspect and recommended-value lists are likewise behind an OAuth token**: `getItemAspectsForCategory` for nodes 41511, 360, 28009, 551 and 38237 would close that gap in one call. In the meantime, two workarounds do work and scale — eBay's `/b/` pages are 403 but **their browse-node titles are in the search index and each title is literally an aspect cross** ("Art Deco Trees & Forest Home Décor Posters & Prints", "Canvas Trees & Forest…", "Signed Original Art Prints"), and `ebay.co.uk/str/<slug>` is reachable with each store's section menu in its page JSON under `STORE_CATEGORY`. One correction to carry forward: **eBay UK node 360 is Art Prints, branded "Pictures And Prints" in the UK, inside the Art branch (550); the Home Décor poster node is 41511, inside Home Décor (10033)**, and **both are leaves**, so all subject and style differentiation on eBay happens through aspects rather than through any deeper category tree ([eBay UK Category Changes](https://pages.ebay.co.uk/categorychanges/UK_Category_Changes.html)).

Finally, two retailers on the brief no longer exist as specified: `artrepublic.com` now 301-redirects to `artfinder.com` after the Brighton gallery lineage closed permanently, and Scandinavian Design Center has been absorbed into Nordic Nest.

## Conclusion

The harvest changes one assumption worth stating. The wall-art market's *collection* taxonomies are additive, not multiplicative — Fy! fills roughly one cell in 10¹² of its own theoretical axis product — but its *product* catalogues are not: Displate carries 1.5 to 2.5 million designs and Society6 reports over 800,000 products in a single theme. The lesson is that the multiplication should not happen between retail merchandising axes, which collapse into near-duplicates when crossed, but between **official subject registries and the artist-free technique axis**, which is where genuine visual difference lives. Eight and a half thousand enumerated British subjects crossed with 139 copyright-free style tokens is 1.19 million designs with no two cells sharing both a subject and a surface, and the extended registries — 10,000 tartans, 70,847 RHS plant names, 282 Munros, 88 constellations — take it well past that without inventing anything. The axes the market leaves deliberately flat (160 personas, place, colour) are the open ground; the axes it crosses densely (base × medium at 17.7%, room × position at 27%) are where the near-duplication risk concentrates, and those are precisely the two to measure before upload.

The build order follows from one component rather than from the taxonomy. **The near-duplicate index is the keystone, and it should exist before generation runs at scale**, because the same HNSW index over CLIP embeddings is simultaneously the dedup gate, the online Poisson-disk cell selector and the source of the Vendi-score measurement that answers "how many of these are actually distinct". Build it first, run it online rather than as a batch audit, and calibrate the 0.85–0.93 band by hand on 300–500 labelled pairs, because that band is where the business lives and the literature is silent. Then two cheap pilots decide the rest: a 2,000-cell step-count A/B per style, and a 400-image upscaler trial on watercolour and impasto printed at A2. Everything else in this specification is downstream of those three measurements, and all of it is more reliable than the published throughput numbers, which conflict by a factor of six and should not be planned against.
