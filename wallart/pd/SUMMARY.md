## Notes

- UK copyright: artist death year must be <= 1955. With no death year, a named artist's work must be dated <= 1880 (and artist born <= 1880); an anonymous work <= 1900. Records with no date at all are excluded.
- Met and Rijksmuseum metadata give no pixel sizes, so they were probed (`--probe-sizes`: Rijksmuseum IIIF info.json, Met JPEG header via a 64 KB range request). Their `too_small` count is applied after `low_demand`; the few that could not be probed keep null sizes.
- NGA IIIF delivers at most 4096 px; recorded sizes are capped to that.
- AIC IIIF needs an explicit width (`/full/{w},/0/default.jpg`) and an `AIC-User-Agent` header.
- Cleveland `image_url` is the full TIFF; `image_url_jpg` is the 3400 px print JPG.
- Rijksmuseum titles are mostly Dutch-only. Simple ones are translated with a small glossary; otherwise the title is built from the English Iconclass subject ("Hare Antique Etching") and only for animal, botanical, landscape, sea, map and poster subjects. The rest is counted under no_english_title.
- Library of Congress posters: "No known restrictions on publication" is an advisory, not a licence; the UK death-year rule still applies. Some LoC sizes are estimated from TIFF file size (`size_estimated: true`).
- YCBA `image_url` is the IIIF full image from the object's manifest.
- Titles never contain museum names; every title passed `compliance.check`.
