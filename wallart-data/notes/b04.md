# Batch 04 — the poster architectures

## 0174 "Capri Italy 1 Fauvist Painting Travel Poster" (3:4)
COMPOSITION — the **framed view** again, but from a terrace rather than a
window. Vertical framing: wall right, railing left, receding tiled floor
leading the eye out to the sea. Potted bougainvillea frames the opening. A
bistro table and **two empty chairs** — the invitation device: you could sit
there.
TYPE — different architecture from Cairngorms and worth noting: the lettering
sits **ON the image**, not in the margin. "ITALY" in bold condensed sans caps
knocked out white across the sky; "Capri" in a white handwritten script at the
foot. Country large, place small — the same two-tier naming, placed differently.
A thin cream margin still surrounds everything.
TECHNIQUE — **Fauvist gouache**: bold unmixed colour, visible separate strokes,
no blending, dark blue-purple outlines around forms. The signature move is
**coloured shadows** — the terrace shadows are magenta and violet, not grey.
Shadow as hue, not as darkness.
PALETTE — cobalt sea and shutters against terracotta-apricot floor
(**complementary blue/orange for the fourth time**), magenta bougainvillea,
emerald foliage, cream walls.
WHY IT WORKS — holiday memory and aspiration; the empty chairs invite the
viewer in; the travel-poster format is a recognised collectable genre.
NOTE — a faint painted signature sits bottom-right. Fy! sells artist work.
**Ours must carry none, and the OCR gate is what enforces that.**
UK TRANSPOSITION — this exact grammar with a harbour instead of a bay:
a terrace or doorway view over Whitby, St Ives, Tenby, Portloe, Staithes. The
competitor corpus has five UK-beach linocuts in total. This is open ground.

## 0144 "Rustic November Birth Flower Chrysanthemum" (3:4) — **the best template**
COMPOSITION — fully structured poster. Generous cream margin. **Three type
zones**: a top line of small letterspaced caps split left and right
("NO.11 // NOVEMBER" | "HONESTY / LOYALTY / LUCK"), an inset rectangular art
panel, then the name "CHRYSANTHEMUM" in large serif caps nearly full width at
the foot.
TECHNIQUE — **linocut**, and correctly observed: pure two-tone, no half-tones
anywhere, and **the white lines are the cut marks** where the block was gouged.
Visible chatter and speckle in the black areas from imperfect inking. Bold
simplified forms.
PALETTE — **three colours total**: cream margin, dusty-rose panel ground, black
ink. Cheap, striking, and fits any interior.
WHY IT WORKS — **a birth flower is a personal gift.** It is month-addressed, so
there are twelve guaranteed variants and each one is a present for somebody
born then. The meanings line adds sentiment. And linocut is the single most
under-supplied technique in the whole competitor corpus — 537 listings across
1.46 million.
WHY IT IS THE BEST FIT FOR US — the generated area is the smallest of any
image seen, and **everything else is type, which our renderer already sets
perfectly**. Minimum diffusion risk, maximum multiplication:
birth flowers x 12 months, zodiac flowers, anniversary flowers, **county
flowers** (a UK angle nobody is using), national flowers, and UK native
wildflowers across a dozen ground colours.
The scaffold `{index} {month} / {meanings} / {panel} / {NAME}` is reusable far
beyond flowers: birthstones, saints' days, counties, breeds, constellations.

## Principle confirmed across seven images
**Complementary blue/orange carries four of seven.** It is not a coincidence,
it is the house trick for making a print read across a room.
