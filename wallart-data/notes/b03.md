# Batch 03 — a new grammar each time

## 0087 "Bruscetta Blue Checkerboard 1" (3:4) — FOOD
COMPOSITION — **flat lay, overhead. No horizon, no depth at all.** Two objects
placed off-centre on a diagonal at roughly the thirds, with generous empty
space between them. **The patterned ground does the composing**: a checkerboard
running edge to edge.
TECHNIQUE — thick **oil impasto**, loaded brush; each checker square is one to
three strokes with visible ridges and drag marks, and the squares are
deliberately imperfect — uneven edges, colour shifting within a square. The
food is rendered tighter than the ground: **the detail gradient again**.
PALETTE — four blues in the ground (cerulean, navy, white, pale sky) against
orange-red tomato, gold crust, emerald basil. **Complementary blue/orange** —
the third time in five images.
WHY IT WORKS — kitchen art, and a room is a buying trigger. Checkerboard is a
live 2024-26 interiors motif. Painterly ridges read as "real art", not a stock
photo.
NEW DEVICE — **the patterned ground as composition engine**: checkerboard,
gingham, tile, terrazzo, stripe, toile. It fills the frame, supplies texture,
makes the subject pop, and is trivially swappable. Dish x ground x technique.

## 0184 "Cairngorms National Park UK Trail Poster" (3:4) — **the key find**
This is precisely the product our UK plan is aiming at, and it proves the
hybrid architecture is what the market already uses.
COMPOSITION — **inset panel inside a cream margin.** ~8% margin at the sides
and top, a deeper ~12% band at the bottom carrying the type. Type is
"CAIRNGORMS NATIONAL PARK" in letterspaced serif caps with "SCOTLAND" smaller
beneath. Two-line hierarchy: place large, region small. The margin is cream,
not white — it reads as paper.
**This is generate-the-panel, composite-the-type, already in the wild.** The
panel is painted; the lettering is clean vector set in the margin. Our
typography renderer does that half perfectly and FLUX does the other half.
TECHNIQUE — **wet-on-wet watercolour**: soft bleeds, blooms, pigment pooling
at the edges of washes. Atmospheric recession, distant hills dissolving into
the sky. Conifers are dark spiky silhouettes with fine ink trunks laid **on
top** of the dry wash — wash first, line last. The river is **reserved white
paper**, not painted, which is the giveaway of real watercolour method.
PALETTE — autumn: burnt orange, amber and gold as the mass; plum and wine in
the foreground scrub; charcoal-green conifers; pale grey-blue distance; cream
sky and margin.
WHY IT WORKS — named UK place, aspirational landscape, poster format: a
memento. The type makes it searchable. Autumn reads cosy to a UK buyer.
NEW AXIS — **SEASON**. The same valley in spring, summer, autumn and winter is
four genuinely different pictures, not four filters.
REUSABLE AS — `{UK National Park / National Landscape / trail}, wet-on-wet
watercolour panel, {season} palette, cream margin, place name composited`.
15 National Parks x 46 National Landscapes x 4 seasons x ~8 techniques is
already in the thousands, and the geometry is loose enough for FLUX.

## Running principles (5 of 6 images so far)
- **Complementary colour pair** carries the image: orange/blue three times.
- **Detail gradient**: subject sharp, surroundings flat. Every single image.
- **No photographic rendering anywhere.** Every mark is a made mark.
- **No gradients** except deliberate watercolour bleed.
- **Flat or patterned grounds** rather than rendered space, except in the
  landscapes.
