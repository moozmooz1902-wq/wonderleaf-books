# Batch 02 — bestsellers continued

## 0083 "Helvellyn England 2 Mountain Painting" (3:4)
**Same subject category as 0082 (a UK fell) but a completely different picture.
This is the proof that subject x technique multiplies without duplicating.**
COMPOSITION — **first-person, looking ALONG the ridge** (Striding Edge), not
across a valley. The ridge runs from bottom-centre into the distance as a
converging V. The viewer is ON the mountain. Horizon ~10% from the top, sky a
pale wash.
TECHNIQUE — broad **palette-knife / flat-brush blocking**. Angular facets with
hard, knife-straight edges. Each facet one flat colour, lightly modulated,
strokes running with the slope. **No outlines at all** — unlike 0080's contour
lines and 0082's hatching. The edge quality is the whole identity.
PALETTE — burnt orange/terracotta on the sunlit flanks against petrol-navy in
shadow: a straight **complementary orange/blue pair**, which is why it punches.
Plus cream-bone for snow and lit rock, sage and pale teal as relief.
WHY IT WORKS — Helvellyn is one of the most-walked mountains in England, so
this is a trophy purchase: "I stood there." The first-person viewpoint sells
the memory, not the view. Bold enough to hold a wall at large sizes.
REUSABLE AS — `{UK peak}, angular palette-knife facets, complementary orange
and petrol blue, first-person ridge view`.

## 0086 "Bird With A Flower Crown Budgerigar 4" (3:4)
**The most efficiently generatable template in the whole sample.** Their own
version of it runs to only 296 listings across 96 species, and leans American.
COMPOSITION — portrait bust, **dead centre, flat ground, nothing else**.
Subject cropped by the bottom edge. Head in the upper third, body filling the
lower two-thirds. The flower crown arches over the skull as a literal halo.
**Direct eye contact with the viewer.**
TECHNIQUE — fine coloured-pencil/gouache with visible directional strokes
through the plumage; feather groups drawn as scalloped overlapping shapes;
flowers rendered at botanical-plate precision. The background is **absolutely
flat single-colour teal** — no texture, no gradient, no vignette.
That flat ground is doing the heavy lifting: it isolates the subject, makes the
colour sing, and keeps the file trivially scalable.
PALETTE — flat teal ground against pink/coral/crimson crown and breast,
yellow-gold and lime at the cheek, sage-mint feather scallops. Highly
saturated, held together by the single complementary ground.
WHY IT WORKS — anthropomorphic charm without cartooning: the bird is dignified,
*wearing* a crown. Eye contact creates a relationship. Readable at any size.
Flat ground suits any wall colour.
REUSABLE AS — fixed composition + fixed lighting + fixed flat ground, **swap
the species**: 636 BOU birds, 221 Kennel Club dogs, 45 cats, 107 mammals,
native livestock. The crown can vary by season or region (bluebells, heather,
gorse) which doubles as a UK differentiator.

## The three axes becoming visible
1. **SUBJECT** — the named thing (Helvellyn, budgerigar, Snowdonia).
2. **TECHNIQUE** — contour hatching / palette knife / cut-paper collage /
   contoured gouache with outlines. Genuinely changes the picture, not a filter.
3. **VIEWPOINT** — across a valley · along a ridge · portrait bust · framed
   through a window. This is the axis their catalogues barely use, and it is
   free: it costs nothing to specify and it changes the image completely.

Subject x technique x viewpoint is the multiplication that produces millions
WITHOUT near-duplicates, because all three change the pixels.
